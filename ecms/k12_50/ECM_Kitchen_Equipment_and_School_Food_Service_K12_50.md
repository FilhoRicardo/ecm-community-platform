# ECM: School Food-Service Equipment and Workflow Optimization

## Summary
Right-size and modernize cafeteria kitchen equipment so process loads fall without disrupting meal delivery.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: meaningful process-load reduction in schools with full kitchen service.
- **End-Uses Affected**: kitchen electricity/gas and associated ventilation loads.

## Basis / References
- The 50% K-12 guide includes dedicated kitchen equipment and design guidance.
- School kitchens are a distinctive process load that can dominate beyond classroom systems.

## Assumptions
Most useful where the school has substantial cooking, holding, or warewashing activity.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with demand-controlled hood ventilation, DHW strategy, and scheduling.

## Implementation Essentials
- Match equipment type and count to actual meal program demand.
- Eliminate oversized or redundant appliances where service model does not justify them.
- Review kitchen schedules against actual serving windows.
- Coordinate replacement with food-service staff and hood design.

## Risks / Constraints
- Throughput or menu changes can invalidate undersized solutions.
- Staff acceptance matters as much as efficiency ratings.

## KPIs
- Kitchen kWh and gas
- Appliance runtime
- Throughput/service performance
- Hood fan hours

## M&V Plan
- Compare kitchen utility use before/after and normalize for meal counts.
- Track runtime of major appliances where practical.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate to high; strongest during kitchen renovation cycles.

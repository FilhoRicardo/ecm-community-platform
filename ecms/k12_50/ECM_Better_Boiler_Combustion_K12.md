# ECM: Better Boiler Combustion Efficiency

## Summary
Install high-efficiency condensing boilers with O2 trim controls, cascade sequencing, and outdoor-reset curves so each school boiler operates near its rated combustion efficiency year-round. Non-condensing boilers in schools typically average 70–80% combustion efficiency; upgrading to condensing boilers at 90–96% efficiency with modern controls reduces heating fuel consumption by 12–22% in cold climates (CIBSE Guide F:2021, Sections 5 and 7).

## Estimated Savings
- **Combustion efficiency gain**: 12–22% reduction in space-heating fuel consumption in Climate Zones 4–8 (CIBSE Guide F:2021, Table 7.2 — Gas-Fired Condensing Boiler Field Efficiency).
- **Part-load efficiency**: cascade control and staging maintain boilers near peak efficiency across heating load range; saves an additional 4–8% vs. single-boiler on/off operation (ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Section 6.8.3 — Boiler Turndown Ratio Requirements).
- **Outdoor-reset control**: matching supply water temperature to load reduces boiler cycling losses by 3–6% (ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Section 6.5.3 — Space Heating Control).
- **End-Uses Affected**: space heating boiler, indirectly DHW heating if a dual-use boiler is installed.

## Basis / References
**CIBSE**
- CIBSE Guide F:2021, Section 5.4: Condensing Boiler Efficiency — rated combustion efficiency 90–96% (LHV basis) for condensing boilers vs. 70–80% for atmospheric non-condensing boilers.
- CIBSE Guide F:2021, Table 7.2: Field-Measured Efficiency by Boiler Type — condensing boilers in schools show 88–94% efficiency in field measurements; non-condensing cast iron boilers show 68–76%.
- CIBSE Guide F:2021, Section 7.3: Cascade Control and Boiler Staging — multiple smaller boilers in cascade outperform single oversized boiler across part-load conditions.

**ASHRAE**
- ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Section 6.8.1: Minimum Efficiency Requirements for Boilers — mandates minimum 90% AFUE for gas-fired boilers ≤ 300,000 Btu/h and 94% for larger condensing types.
- ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Section 6.8.3: Boiler Turndown — requires minimum 5:1 turndown for boilers > 300,000 Btu/h to avoid short-cycling losses.
- ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Section 6.5.3: Outdoor Reset Control — requires supply water temperature reset based on outdoor air temperature.
- ASHRAE 189.3-2018 Section 7.2.2: High-Efficiency Boiler Plant — recommends condensing boiler technology for new school buildings in Climate Zones 4 and above.

**Other**
- AEDG50-K12-2011.pdf: Recommends right-sizing boilers usingocl load calculations per ASHRAE Handbook — HVAC Applications; specifies minimum 90% efficiency for gas-fired heating plant.

## Assumptions
- School heating plant is a gas-fired boiler serving perimeter radiation or air-handling units; the existing boiler is non-condensing atmospheric or forced-draft type > 10 years old.
- Heating plant serves a building envelope that meets or exceeds ASHRAE 90.1-2019 (or local code equivalent) for wall R-values and window U-factors; the envelope is not the primary savings driver.
- Natural gas unit cost: $0.60–$1.20/therm (varies by region); heating load profile: 2,000–5,000 heating degree days (HDD) per year.

## Climate Zone Relevance
- **Climate Zones 4–8**: highest priority; condensing boiler efficiency gain is greatest when return water temperature can be kept below 130°F (enabling condensation); savings 15–22%.
- **Climate Zones 2–3**: condensing boilers still applicable but savings are smaller (8–15%) because heating season is shorter and return water temperatures may not drop enough to sustain condensation.
- **Climate Zone 1**: marginal benefit; conventional high-efficiency boilers may be more cost-effective.

## Interaction Notes
- Complements HVAC Optimization ECM: better boiler efficiency improves the benefit of reducing simultaneous heating/cooling conflicts.
- Outdoor-reset control requires BAS integration with the boiler plant; if the school lacks BAS, a standalone boiler controller with outdoor-air sensor must be added.
- If DHW is generated by the same boiler, cascade control must preserve DHW priority; ensure HW supply temperature during summer mode meets DHW needs (typically 140°F final temp) while still allowing condensing operation.

## Implementation Essentials
1. Perform a heating load calculation per ASHRAE Handbook — Fundamentals (2021), Chapter 18, to right-size the replacement boiler(s); do not replace on a like-for-like size basis if the existing boiler is oversized.
2. Select condensing boilers with minimum 95% efficiency (LHV), 5:1 or better turndown ratio, and built-in O2 trim or combustion-air damper control.
3. Install multiple smaller boilers (2 or 3 units) in cascade rather than a single large boiler; size each unit at 50–75% of peak load so one boiler handles mild-weather loads and staging handles cold-weather peak.
4. Install an outdoor-air temperature sensor and configure outdoor-reset supply water temperature curves in the boiler controller per manufacturer specifications; typical reset schedule: 180°F at 0°F OA, 140°F at 40°F OA, 120°F at 60°F OA.
5. Add O2 trim controls to each boiler if not factory-installed; maintain O2 setpoint at 3.5–5.0% (natural gas) to ensure complete combustion without excess air penalties.
6. Integrate boiler plant with BAS for cascade sequencing, alarm management, and runtime trending; minimum 15-minute data resolution for boiler runtime, firing rate, and supply/return water temperatures.
7. Insulate all HW distribution piping to minimum R-8 per ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Table 6.8.3-1; inspect existing pipe insulation and replace any damaged or missing sections discovered during boiler replacement.
8. Set up lead-lag rotation so each boiler in the cascade alternates as lead unit on a weekly basis; this balances runtime and prevents one boiler from bearing disproportionate wear.

## Risks / Constraints
**Failure Mode — Oversizing Replacement Boiler**: installing a boiler sized for worst-case design day conditions (rather than right-sized for actual load profile) causes chronic short-cycling, which drops seasonal efficiency by 8–12% below rated efficiency.

**Mitigation**: use bin-temperature or degree-day load calculation; size boiler(s) so the smallest boiler in the cascade handles ≥ 30% of peak load without short-cycling; verify turndown ratio matches minimum load condition.

**Failure Mode — Return Water Temperature Too High for Condensation**: if the heating system uses high-temperature emitters (older cast-iron radiation without carefully sized surface area) or if system is piped incorrectly (no decoupling), return water may stay above 130°F, preventing the boiler from entering condensing mode.

**Mitigation**: verify system supply/return temperature differential (ΔT) at design conditions is ≥ 20°F; if return water temp exceeds 130°F at design heating load, reconfigure or add low-temperature emitters (radiant floor or fan-coils) to lower return temperature; commissioning agent must verify condensing mode operation.

**Failure Mode — DHW Priority Conflicts with Condensing Mode**: dual-use boiler serving both space heating and DHW may see its return water temperature elevated by DHW heat exchanger, limiting condensing operation in shoulder seasons.

**Mitigation**: use a dedicated DHW boiler or configure priority DHW mode that isolates space heating return water during DHW draw; consider indirect DHW with dedicated tank heated by condensing boiler on a schedule.

## KPIs
- Boiler seasonal efficiency (%) — target: ≥ 92% average combustion efficiency across heating season (IPMVP Option B, fuel input vs. heat output measurement).
- Annual heating fuel consumption (therms/year or Btu/ft²/year) — target: 20–35% reduction vs. pre-retrofit 3-year average, weather-normalized.
- Boiler runtime hours per heating season per unit — track to identify abnormal short-cycling; target: < 2,400 full-load equivalent hours/year for a properly sized boiler in Climate Zone 5.
- Supply/return water temperature differential (°F) at design conditions — target: ΔT ≥ 20°F at outdoor design temp.
- O2 level in flue gas (%) — target: 3.5–5.0% for natural gas; document readings at commissioning and quarterly.
- Stack temperature (°F) — target: within 20°F of condensate return temperature for condensing mode operation.

## M&V Plan
- **IPMVP Option**: Option B (Retrofit — Isolated Measure, Heating Plant).
- **Quantification approach**: measure boiler fuel input (therm/hour or Btu/hour via gas meter or burner management system) and heat output (gpm × ΔT × 500 × specific heat) simultaneously during commissioned operating conditions; calculate seasonal efficiency by integrating over the heating season using bin-temperature method (ASHRAE Handbook — Fundamentals, Chapter 18). Compare pre/post weather-normalized annual fuel consumption using 3-year pre-retrofit baseline.
- **Data collection**:
  - Natural gas consumption (hourly, whole-building utility bill with monthly granularity minimum, sub-meter on boiler plant if available).
  - Boiler firing rate and runtime (burner management system trend, 15-minute resolution, continuous).
  - Supply and return water temperatures (BAS trend, 15-minute resolution, continuous).
  - Heating water flow rate (gpm from boiler flow meter or pressure drop calibration, continuous or periodic measurement).
  - Outdoor air temperature (local weather station or on-site sensor, hourly, for HDD normalization).
  - DHW consumption gallons/day (if boiler serves DHW) to normalize summer baseline.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- **Capex**: Condensing boiler plant (2 × 500 MBtu/h cascade): $60,000–$90,000 including boiler, venting, gas train, controls, and installation. O2 trim add-on: $3,000–$6,000 per boiler. BAS integration: $5,000–$12,000. Piping and insulation upgrades: $4,000–$10,000.
- **Opex**: $1,500–$3,000/year for annual combustion analysis, O2 sensor replacement, and boiler service contract.
- **Simple Payback**: 4.5–8.0 years based on 15–22% heating fuel reduction (valued at $0.08–$0.12/kBtu equivalent) in Climate Zones 5–8. Shorter payback (3.5–5 years) when boiler is oversized > 40% and fuel costs exceed $1.00/therm.
- **ROI**: 12–22% over 10 years.

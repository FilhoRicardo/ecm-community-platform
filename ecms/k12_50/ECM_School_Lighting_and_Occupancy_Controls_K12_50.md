# ECM: School Lighting Retrofit with Occupancy and Daylight Controls

## Summary
Use lower-LPD lighting with school-appropriate occupancy and daylight controls so classrooms, support spaces, and admin areas are not overlit or left on unnecessarily.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: major electricity reduction strategy in the 50% K-12 guide.
- **Typical effect**: strong whole-building savings where older lighting and poor scheduling are common.
- **End-Uses Affected**: interior lighting and secondary cooling.

## Basis / References
- The guide includes interior/exterior lighting, occupancy sensor discussion, and school-specific application guidance.
- Classroom, office, corridor, and support spaces should not all be controlled the same way.

## Assumptions
Requires school-space-specific design rather than a district-wide generic fixture/control standard.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Works with daylighting, task-based lighting, and after-hours school use policies.

## Implementation Essentials
- Set LPD and control logic by room type.
- Use occupancy/vacancy sensors where appropriate and schedules for after-hours functions.
- Commission photosensors and dimming in daylit spaces.
- Revisit controls after classroom technology or furniture changes.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor tuning creates teacher/staff resistance.
- Custodial and after-hours community use can undermine schedules if not considered.

## KPIs
- Lighting kWh by building wing or area type
- After-hours lighting runtime
- Override rates
- Teacher/staff complaints

## M&V Plan
- Compare panel or wing-level lighting energy before/after.
- Review sensor and daylight-control trends.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Usually favorable, especially in older schools.

# ECM: Match School HVAC System Type to Program, Climate, and Operability

## Summary
Select HVAC architecture based on school use, climate, ventilation needs, and district operations capability rather than defaulting to familiar but inefficient systems.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: system-path measure; large effect on heating, cooling, fan, and ventilation energy.
- **Typical effect**: major whole-building difference depending on whether the chosen system fits school schedules and zoning.
- **End-Uses Affected**: whole HVAC.

## Basis / References
- The 50% K-12 guide includes HVAC system types, system considerations, and sidebars on VAV/DOAS and GSHP.
- Good school HVAC performance depends as much on simplicity/operability as on nameplate efficiency.

## Assumptions
Best addressed before design development is advanced.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Depends heavily on daylighting, envelope, occupancy patterns, and district maintenance capacity.

## Implementation Essentials
- Compare system options against actual school use patterns and district O&M skills.
- Avoid selecting complex systems the owner cannot maintain.
- Keep ventilation, humidity, and zoning responsibilities explicit.
- Re-run loads after envelope and lighting reductions.

## Risks / Constraints
- Operators may disable advanced sequences if systems are too complex.
- Familiar system bias can override lifecycle logic.

## KPIs
- HVAC EUI
- Fan energy
- Ventilation energy
- Comfort and IAQ complaint rate

## M&V Plan
- Use whole-building HVAC trend review and subsystem data where available.
- Compare actual operation with intended sequences in the first year.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- High design leverage; economics vary by chosen architecture.


## Templates / Reuse
- ECM screening worksheet
- Climate-zone applicability note
- KPI / M&V checklist

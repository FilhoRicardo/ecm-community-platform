# ECM: Classroom Sidelighting and Toplighting Package

## Summary
Use classroom daylighting deliberately through sidelighting, toplighting, or both so electric lighting is reduced while visual comfort and teaching quality are preserved.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: one of the strongest school-specific strategies in the 50% K-12 guide.
- **Typical effect**: major daytime lighting reduction when glare and AV conflicts are resolved.
- **End-Uses Affected**: classroom lighting and some secondary cooling.

## Basis / References
- The 50% K-12 guide includes dedicated sections for classroom sidelighting, toplighting, and combined strategies.
- It also includes shading and daylighting examples specific to schools.

## Assumptions
Requires coordination of glazing, teaching walls, AV, shading, and controls.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with occupancy/daylight controls, high-reflectance surfaces, and classroom layout.

## Implementation Essentials
- Select the daylighting approach based on room depth, roof form, and façade access.
- Protect teaching-wall visibility and screen use.
- Diffuse toplighting and shade direct sun.
- Keep daylight-control zones aligned with actual daylight distribution.

## Risks / Constraints
- Without glare control, teachers may permanently disable the strategy.
- Roof apertures require careful maintenance planning.

## KPIs
- Classroom lighting kWh
- Daylight autonomy
- Glare complaints
- Blind closure rates

## M&V Plan
- Use classroom simulation in design.
- Compare classroom lighting runtime and controls behavior after occupancy.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate to high depending on roof/façade scope; often high-value in new schools.

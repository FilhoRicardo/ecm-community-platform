# ECM: RTU Mini-Split Conversion in Guest Rooms

## Summary

Replace single-packaged RTUs serving individual guest rooms with inverter-driven mini-split heat pumps — eliminates duct losses, allows individual temperature control per room, and improves COP. RTUs serving individual rooms are oversized for most loads, cycle inefficiently, and leak conditioned air through ductwork. Mini-splits directly condition the room with 2–3x better efficiency at part-load conditions typical in lodging.

## Estimated Savings
- 15–30% guest room HVAC energy
- 15–25 kBtu/sq ft/yr

## Basis / References
- HPAC Engineering 'Mini-Split Retrofits in Hotels' 2020
- DOE Hospitality Energy Mangement

## Assumptions
- Guest rooms have adequate wall/partition space for head unit installation
- Electrical service capacity available for dedicated circuit per room
- No structural modifications required for refrigerant lines

## Implementation Essentials

1. Survey guest room layouts for outdoor wall space and electrical panel capacity.
2. Select appropriate capacity (9,000–12,000 Btu for typical rooms); inverter-driven units preferred for part-load efficiency.
3. Install condensate line routing to existing drain or exterior.
4. Run dedicated electrical circuit from panel to each room (15–20 amp circuit typical).
5. Replace old RTU with head unit and outdoor condenser — recover and properly dispose of old refrigerant per EPA Section 608.
6. Commission and setpoint configuration — coordinate with guestroom occupancy管理系统.

## Risks / Constraints

- Guest room disruption during construction — schedule during low-occupancy periods
- Aesthetic concerns with wall-mounted units — confirm property manager/guest acceptance
- EPA Section 608 refrigerant recovery requirements for old RTUs
- Seasonal demand may limit installation window

## KPIs
- Guest room HVAC kBtu/sq ft/yr
- Guest complaint rate (temperature)
- RTU runtime hours

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)
- Install whole-building natural gas and electricity meters (IPMVP Option C)
- Submeter guest room level via optional circuit-level monitoring or portable logger deployment
- Baseline: 12-month pre-installation EUI (weather-normalized)
- Post-installation: 12-month EUI comparison using same normalization methodology
- Utility bill analysis per guest room wing if submetering installed

# ECM: Envelope Air Sealing and Glazing Improvement for Guest Comfort

## Summary
Improve air sealing, door weatherstripping, and window/glazing performance to reduce infiltration, perimeter discomfort, and waste HVAC energy in guestrooms and public areas. In older motels and roadside lodging, envelope deficiencies can account for 15–30% of heating and cooling loads (AEDG30-HighwayLodging.pdf, Section 3 — Building Envelope), making envelope improvements a foundational first step before HVAC equipment upgrades.

## Estimated Savings
- **Infiltration reduction**: 10–30% reduction in heating and cooling loads through air sealing in buildings with high baseline infiltration rates (> 0.4 ACH50) (CIBSE Guide B:2019, Table 3.1).
- **Glazing upgrade**: 5–15% reduction in perimeter heating/cooling loads when single-pane windows are replaced with double-pane low-E glazing (ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Table 5.5.4.2.1.
- **Combined envelope improvement**: 8–20% whole-building HVAC energy reduction in cold and mixed climates; 5–12% in hot-humid climates.
- **End-Uses Affected**: heating, cooling, and guest comfort in perimeter zones.

## Basis / References
**CIBSE**
- CIBSE Guide B:2019, Table 3.1: Air leakage rates and infiltration reduction potentials for building assemblies — indicates 10–30% load reduction achievable in tight buildings.
- CIBSE Guide B:2019, Section 4.3: Windows and Glazing — recommends U-factor ≤ 0.30 Btu/hr·ft²·°F for climate zones 4–8.

**ASHRAE**
- ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Table 5.5.4.2.1: Maximum Assembly U-factors for Fenestration — sets maximum window U-factor of 0.30 for climate zones 4–8 and 0.60 for climate zones 1–3.
- ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Section 5.4.3: Air Sealing — requires air barrier continuity at all penetrations and junctions.
- ASHRAE Handbook — Fundamentals, Chapter 16: Fenestration — provides glazing thermal performance methodology.

**Other**
- AEDG30-HighwayLodging.pdf, Section 3: Building Envelope — includes roof, wall, door, and glazing recommendations by climate zone; emphasizes early load reduction as the first step in the integrated design sequence.

## Assumptions
- Building is conditioned (guestrooms and common areas are heated and/or cooled).
- Baseline window condition is single-pane or original glazing with no low-E coating.
- Infiltration is primarily through operable windows, door perimeters, sleeve penetrations, and envelope joints rather than mechanical ventilation.

## Climate Zone Relevance
- **Cold climates (Zones 6–8)**: Highest savings from infiltration sealing and glazing upgrade; heating degree days dominate envelope loads.
- **Hot-humid climates (Zones 1–3)**: Glazing solar heat-gain coefficient (SHGC) and air sealing are most valuable; cooling loads benefit from reduced moisture infiltration.
- **Mixed climates (Zones 4–5)**: Both heating and cooling savings apply; balanced approach recommended.

## Interaction Notes
- Reduces the heating and cooling loads that PTAC/PTHP units must meet, making occupancy-based setback controls more effective and faster to recover.
- Supports guestroom bathroom exhaust optimization by reducing the amount of outdoor air that must be exhausted against infiltration.
- Glazing upgrades are most cost-effective when coincident with room refresh or exterior renovation.

## Implementation Essentials
1. Conduct a baseline blower-door test (ASTM E779 or E1827) on a representative guestroom block to establish ACH50; target ≤ 0.3 ACH50 for renovated guestrooms per ASHRAE Standard 100-2018 Section 5.3.
2. Audit window seals, door sweeps, sleeve penetrations, and façade joint locations using infrared thermography (ASTM C1060) during压差 test conditions.
3. Seal all identified leakage paths: apply weatherstripping to guestroom entry doors (perimeter and bottom sweep), seal window frames, close unused sleeve penetrations, and air-seal roof-to-wall junctions in corridors.
4. Replace glazing where window condition is poor (U-factor > 0.50, single-pane, or failed seal): install double-pane low-E windows with U-factor ≤ 0.30 and SHGC ≤ 0.25 for climate zones 4–8 per ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Table 5.5.4.2.1.
5. Install exterior door sweeps and thresholds (or replace doors where thermal performance is severely compromised): target U-factor ≤ 0.50 for opaque doors per ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Table 5.5.4.2.2.
6. Address corridor pressurization: ensure that envelope sealing in guestrooms does not negatively pressurize corridors relative to adjacent unconditioned spaces, which can increase corridor HVAC loads.
7. Post-work verification: repeat blower-door test on treated guestrooms and confirm ≥ 30% ACH50 reduction; conduct IR scan to verify sealed locations.

## Risks / Constraints
**Failure Mode — Moisture Condensation in Envelope Cavity**: Aggressive air sealing without adequate vapor control can trap moisture in wall assemblies, causing mold growth and reduced insulation effectiveness.

**Mitigation**: Ensure interior vapor retarder is continuous at drywall seams; verify that wall assemblies can dry inward; monitor for condensation on interior surfaces during winter months in cold climates.

**Failure Mode — Pressure Imbalance Between Guestrooms and Corridors**: Over-sealing guestroom entry doors can reverse airflow direction, pushing conditioned corridor air into unconditioned spaces and increasing corridor HVAC loads.

**Mitigation**: Test pressure relationships with a manometer; if negative pressure exceeds 5 Pa, add minimal exhaust relief or adjust corridor pressurization; monitor corridor differential seasonally.

**Failure Mode — Incomplete Sleeve and Frame Sealing**: One-for-one window replacement without addressing the sleeve, frame, and rough-opening gaps can preserve leakage paths around the new window.

**Mitigation**: Specify full-frame replacement rather than insert-frame replacement; require air-sealing of the rough opening per ASTM E2112 prior to window installation.

## KPIs
- ACH50 post-sealing (air changes per hour at 50 Pa) — target: ≤ 0.3 ACH50 for renovated guestrooms.
- Window U-factor (Btu/hr·ft²·°F) — target: ≤ 0.30 in climate zones 4–8; ≤ 0.60 in climate zones 1–3.
- Perimeter comfort complaints per 100 room-nights — target: < 0.3 complaints/100 room-nights post-renovation.
- Heating EUI (kBtu/ft²/year) — target: 10–25% reduction from pre-sealing baseline in climate zones 5–8.
- Cooling EUI (kBtu/ft²/year) — target: 5–15% reduction from pre-sealing baseline in climate zones 1–4.
- Identified leakage defects resolved (%) — target: 100% of identified defects resolved in treated rooms.

## M&V Plan
- **IPMVP Option**: Option A (Isolation, Key Component) for envelope elements; supplemented with Option C (Whole Building) for overall HVAC energy impact.
- **Quantification approach**: Compare pre/post room-block heating and cooling energy normalized for weather (HDD/CDD) and occupancy. Use ASHRAE 90.1-2019 Appendix D weather normalization procedure. Post-construction blower-door test provides direct measurement of infiltration improvement.
- **Data collection**:
  - Blower-door test results (ACH50) before and after sealing, by room type.
  - IR scan images documenting leakage locations before and after work.
  - Guestroom HVAC runtime ( BAS trend or standalone logger, 15-minute resolution).
  - Monthly utility consumption by fuel type (electricity, natural gas) for treated room block.
  - Daily occupied room-nights for normalization.
  - HDD and CDD from nearest ASHRAE climatic data station.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- **Capex (air sealing)**: $800–$2,500 per guestroom (weatherstripping, sealant, IR diagnostics, labor); $3,000–$8,000 per 100-room property.
- **Capex (glazing replacement)**: $350–$600 per window (double-pane low-E, full-frame replacement) for a typical 3×5 ft guestroom window; $12,000–$20,000 per 100-room property for full window replacement.
- **Opex**: $200–$400/year for post-work inspection and verification.
- **Simple Payback**: 3–7 years for air sealing alone; 6–12 years for glazing replacement (glazing payback improves to 4–8 years when combined with planned room renovation or exterior maintenance cycle).
- **ROI**: 14–33% over 10 years.

# ECM: Guestroom and Bathroom Ventilation Optimization

## Summary
Reduce unnecessary outside-air and exhaust energy while maintaining moisture control and IAQ in guestrooms, bathrooms, and corridors.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: moderate HVAC savings through lower ventilation-related load and fan runtime.
- **End-Uses Affected**: ventilation heating/cooling and exhaust fan electricity.

## Basis / References
- The lodging AEDG treats ventilation control as one of the “bonus savings” areas and practical optimization themes.
- Quality assurance and commissioning are emphasized because poor ventilation settings can erase savings.

## Assumptions
Applicable where constant operation or excessive OA is common.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones, with strongest value in hot-humid and cold climates.

## Interaction Notes
Supports corridor pressurization, guestroom humidity control, and efficient guestroom HVAC operation.

## Implementation Essentials
- Review bathroom exhaust schedules and demand assumptions.
- Avoid overventilation of corridors and support spaces.
- Balance OA intake with actual occupancy and code needs.
- Recommission pressure relationships after changes.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor balance can create odor transfer or humidity issues.
- Guest comfort and bathroom moisture control must remain acceptable.

## KPIs
- Exhaust fan runtime
- Corridor pressure relationship
- Ventilation-related HVAC energy
- Humidity complaints

## M&V Plan
- Trend fan runtime and space pressure before/after changes.
- Compare seasonal HVAC energy in affected areas.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Often low to moderate, especially when control changes dominate.

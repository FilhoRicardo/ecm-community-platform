# ECM: LED Lighting with Guestroom and Exterior Controls

## Summary
Upgrade interior and exterior lighting to LED and apply room-level, corridor, and site controls so lighting energy falls without reducing guest experience or safety.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: lighting is a major driver of lodging energy use.
- **Typical effect**: strong electricity reduction when legacy lamps and uncontrolled common-area lighting are present.
- **End-Uses Affected**: interior lighting, exterior/site lighting, and secondary cooling.

## Basis / References
- The lodging guide treats interior and exterior lighting as core recommendation areas.
- The guide also emphasizes practical implementation and quality assurance rather than purely theoretical design.

## Assumptions
Most useful where guestrooms, corridors, lobby, and site lighting still rely on older lamp technologies or poor schedules.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Reduces cooling load slightly and pairs well with occupancy-based guestroom control.

## Implementation Essentials
- Replace guestroom, corridor, lobby, and exterior fixtures with suitable LED products.
- Use occupancy or vacancy logic in low-use support areas.
- Apply astronomical or photocell scheduling to exterior lighting.
- Rebalance lighting levels so efficiency does not compromise visual quality.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor LED selection can create color-quality complaints in guestrooms.
- Exterior-lighting reductions must be coordinated with safety and brand standards.

## KPIs
- Lighting kWh
- Exterior lighting runtime
- Guestroom lighting load per room
- Guest complaints related to lighting quality

## M&V Plan
- Compare lighting panel or site-electricity trends pre/post upgrade.
- Verify scheduling and after-hours runtime.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Usually favorable, especially where lamp maintenance burden is high.

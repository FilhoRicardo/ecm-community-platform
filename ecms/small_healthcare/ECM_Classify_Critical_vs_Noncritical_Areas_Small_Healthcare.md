# ECM: Classify Critical vs Noncritical Areas and Match HVAC Intensity Accordingly

## Summary
Separate truly critical-care or higher-intensity spaces from noncritical areas so ventilation, pressure, temperature, and system complexity are not overapplied everywhere.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: major design-phase savings through lower airflow, lower reheat, and simpler HVAC in eligible spaces.
- **End-Uses Affected**: ventilation, heating, cooling, and fan energy.

## Basis / References
- The healthcare AEDG repeatedly distinguishes between critical and noncritical areas and allows different system pathways accordingly.
- System type applicability is a key healthcare-specific design issue.

## Assumptions
Best addressed in early programming and schematic design.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Shapes the viability of VAV, fan-coil, WSHP, and setback measures throughout the project.

## Implementation Essentials
- Review each space against actual clinical function and required pressure/air-change criteria.
- Limit highest-intensity air-side strategies to spaces that truly require them.
- Separate support/admin/public areas where lower-energy approaches are acceptable.
- Keep the classification current through design and fit-out changes.

## Risks / Constraints
- Overclassification is common and can lock in permanent energy waste.
- Underclassification creates compliance and safety risk.

## KPIs
- Floor area by criticality category
- OA and airflow rates by department
- Reheat intensity
- Zones using high-intensity HVAC unnecessarily

## M&V Plan
- Primarily design-stage verification via room data sheets and airflow budgets.
- Post-occupancy, compare actual airflow and runtime by classified area.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Planning and design measure with high leverage and low direct capex.

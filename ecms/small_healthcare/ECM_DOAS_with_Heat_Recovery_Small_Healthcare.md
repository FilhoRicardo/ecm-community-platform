# ECM: Dedicated Outdoor Air with Exhaust-Air Energy Recovery

## Summary
Use dedicated outdoor-air treatment with sensible or total energy recovery so healthcare ventilation loads are reduced without compromising air-change and IAQ requirements.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: high-value HVAC package measure because ventilation and reheat are large healthcare end uses.
- **Typical effect**: strong reduction in outdoor-air heating/cooling burden where exhaust streams and code allow.
- **End-Uses Affected**: ventilation heating, cooling, and reheat.

## Basis / References
- The small healthcare AEDG includes DOAS energy recovery requirements that vary by humid and dry subclimates.
- Ventilation air treatment is one of the most important levers in the healthcare package.

## Assumptions
Requires acceptable exhaust-stream pairing, maintenance capability, and a system architecture that separates ventilation from zone sensible loads.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones, especially humid and extreme climates where OA treatment energy is high.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs strongly with fan-coils, WSHP systems, and condenser heat-recovery opportunities.

## Implementation Essentials
- Identify code-acceptable exhaust streams and recovery technology.
- Keep latent and sensible responsibilities clear between DOAS and terminal systems.
- Commission bypass, frost, and humidity-control logic carefully.
- Trend supply-air humidity, recovery effectiveness, and reheat after occupancy.

## Risks / Constraints
- Cross-contamination rules may limit technology choice.
- Poor maintenance can degrade recovery quickly.
- Pressure-drop penalties must be managed.

## KPIs
- DOAS kWh and thermal energy
- Recovery effectiveness
- Reheat energy
- Humidity excursions

## M&V Plan
- Use subsystem metering or trend-derived OA treatment energy comparison.
- Verify humidity and supply-air performance in representative seasons.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate to high, usually justified where OA loads are large and continuous.

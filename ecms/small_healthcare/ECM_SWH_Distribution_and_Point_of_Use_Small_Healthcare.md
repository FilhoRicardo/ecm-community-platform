# ECM: Domestic Hot Water Distribution and Point-of-Use Rationalization

## Summary
Reduce hot-water distribution losses by minimizing dead legs, improving recirculation control, and using point-of-use solutions for remote, low-demand healthcare loads.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: moderate DHW savings with stronger service and infection-control benefits where distribution is excessive.
- **End-Uses Affected**: DHW heating energy and recirculation power.

## Basis / References
- The small healthcare AEDG discusses heater location, recirculation challenges, and point-of-use water heaters for remote low-volume loads.
- Healthcare hot-water systems are often overdistributed due to layout and service expectations.

## Assumptions
Best where demand is distributed across remote sinks, support spaces, or specialty equipment.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with high-efficiency water heating and DHW plant right-sizing. Must be reviewed alongside infection-control and temperature-management requirements.

## Implementation Essentials
- Map DHW demand points and identify unnecessary loop length.
- Use local point-of-use heating for isolated small loads where practical.
- Insulate supply/return piping and review recirculation runtime.
- Maintain delivery temperature and hygiene requirements.

## Risks / Constraints
- Healthcare temperature-management and bacterial-control requirements limit aggressive setback.
- Too many small local heaters can create maintenance burden.

## KPIs
- DHW energy
- Recirculation pump runtime
- Return temperature
- Hot-water wait times at representative fixtures

## M&V Plan
- Compare DHW plant/runtime and loop behavior pre/post changes.
- Track user complaints and delivery performance.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Low to moderate depending on whether this is a piping/control optimization or broader replumbing effort.

# ECM: Condenser Heat Recovery for Reheat and DHW Preheat

## Summary
Recover waste heat from cooling equipment to offset reheat or domestic hot-water loads in small healthcare buildings with simultaneous cooling and heating demand.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: identified as a notable healthcare opportunity because cooling and reheat often occur at the same time.
- **Typical effect**: strongest where ventilation-driven reheat is persistent.
- **End-Uses Affected**: boiler/DHW energy and cooling plant interaction.

## Basis / References
- The small healthcare guide discusses condenser heat recovery as a meaningful hospital/clinic opportunity.
- This is particularly relevant where DOAS reduces but does not eliminate reheat.

## Assumptions
Requires meaningful simultaneous cooling and heating loads and plant temperatures compatible with recovered heat.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones, especially where dehumidification and ventilation drive reheat.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with chilled-water systems, DOAS, and DHW strategies. Benefits may be limited if heating demands are intermittent or low-grade heat is hard to use.

## Implementation Essentials
- Screen concurrent cooling and heating profiles first.
- Define the sink: reheat coil loop, DHW preheat, or both.
- Coordinate plant sequencing and minimum lift requirements.
- Trend recovered heat utilization after startup.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor load matching can weaken economics.
- Control complexity increases and must be commissioned carefully.

## KPIs
- Recovered heat energy
- Boiler fuel reduction
- DHW preheat contribution
- Chiller runtime and lift

## M&V Plan
- Use plant-level metering or calculated heat recovery from temperature/flow trends.
- Compare boiler/DHW energy before and after normalization.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Project-specific; strongest in reheat-heavy facilities.

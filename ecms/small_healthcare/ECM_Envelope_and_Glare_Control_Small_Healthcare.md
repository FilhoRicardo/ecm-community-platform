# ECM: Envelope, Glazing, and Solar-Control Upgrade for Small Healthcare

## Summary
Use better opaque envelope performance, glazing selection, and solar control to reduce perimeter loads while improving patient/staff visual comfort.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: envelope and fenestration are major prescriptive categories in the guide.
- **Typical effect**: material whole-building load reduction plus better daylight usability.
- **End-Uses Affected**: heating, cooling, glare-related lighting use, and perimeter comfort.

## Basis / References
- The small healthcare guide provides climate-zone prescriptions for roof, wall, glazing, and shading performance.
- Daylighting and glazing are linked closely to patient/staff/public-area quality.

## Assumptions
Most useful in new construction or major façade/roof renovation.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones, with stronger solar-control value in hot/mixed climates and stronger U-factor value in colder climates.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with daylight harvesting and lower installed lighting power. Benefits depend on whether blinds remain open and controls are commissioned.

## Implementation Essentials
- Use whole-assembly glazing performance and orientation-specific solar strategy.
- Keep fenestration ratio disciplined rather than using broad curtainwall assumptions.
- Add shading where solar exposure justifies it.
- Revisit perimeter HVAC sizing after envelope changes.

## Risks / Constraints
- Overglazing can reintroduce glare and cooling penalties.
- Clinical privacy and infection-control cleaning may influence product choice.

## KPIs
- Perimeter heating/cooling load
- Daylight autonomy in target spaces
- Glare complaints
- Perimeter comfort complaints

## M&V Plan
- Use design simulation plus post-occupancy trend review in perimeter zones.
- Track lighting and HVAC behavior in daylit spaces.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Variable; strongest when coordinated with planned capital work.

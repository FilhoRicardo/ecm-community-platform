# ECM: Patient, Clinical, and Staff Lighting Optimization

## Summary
Use low-LPD, layered LED lighting with task-appropriate scenes in patient, exam, nurse-station, and treatment spaces rather than blanket high ambient levels.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: lighting is a major whole-building electricity end use in small healthcare.
- **Typical effect**: strong electricity reduction with better scene control and lower cooling from lighting gains.
- **End-Uses Affected**: interior lighting and secondary cooling.

## Basis / References
- The small healthcare AEDG provides space-by-space lighting and control logic for patient rooms, nurse stations, ORs, exam rooms, and treatment spaces.
- The guide treats lighting quality and function as inseparable from energy reduction.

## Assumptions
Requires design by clinical task rather than conservative overlighting.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with daylighting, occupancy controls in eligible rooms, and quiet-at-night strategies.

## Implementation Essentials
- Separate ambient, exam/task, reading, and night-light functions where applicable.
- Use dimming or multiscene controls tailored to care workflows.
- Reduce ambient installed power instead of relying on one high-output layer.
- Commission scene levels with user groups before turnover.

## Risks / Constraints
- Clinical stakeholders may resist lower ambient power without mockups.
- Poor control usability can cause overrides and dissatisfaction.

## KPIs
- Installed LPD by space type
- Lighting kWh
- Scene runtime by mode
- Staff/patient satisfaction feedback

## M&V Plan
- Compare panel or floor lighting energy before/after retrofit or design baseline.
- Review control logs and occupant feedback after occupancy.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate, often favorable when aligned with fixture replacement cycles.

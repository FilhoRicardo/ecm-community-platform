# ECM: Separate Warehouse Office Areas and Apply Office-Appropriate ECMs

## Summary
Treat warehouse office areas as their own energy subprogram and apply office-appropriate envelope, lighting, and controls rather than using warehouse assumptions everywhere.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: moderate but important because office zones are often overconditioned or under-optimized when buried inside warehouse design.
- **End-Uses Affected**: office lighting, HVAC, and plug loads within the warehouse building.

## Basis / References
- The warehouse guide explicitly states that office portions should draw from the small-office AEDG logic.
- Hybrid warehouse-office buildings need different strategies by area type.

## Assumptions
Applicable where office/support area is nontrivial or conditioned differently from storage.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Supports zoning, right-sizing, and office-specific plug/load control.

## Implementation Essentials
- Separate office zones electrically and mechanically where practical.
- Apply office lighting controls, plug-load policy, and comfort bands to office areas.
- Avoid high-bay warehouse criteria in enclosed offices and meeting rooms.
- Benchmark office-area energy separately if metering allows.

## Risks / Constraints
- Office areas are often too small to attract design attention yet large enough to waste energy.
- Mixed schedules between office and warehouse use can create control conflicts.

## KPIs
- Office-area lighting and HVAC energy
- Office after-hours load
- Comfort complaints in office zones
- Separate zone runtime where trended

## M&V Plan
- Use submetering or representative zone trends where available.
- Compare office-area operation against warehouse-area schedules and loads.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Low to moderate, often mainly design and controls discipline.

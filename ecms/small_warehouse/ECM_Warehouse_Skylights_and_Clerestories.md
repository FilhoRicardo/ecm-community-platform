# ECM: Skylights and North-Facing Clerestories for Warehouse Daylighting

## Summary
Use controlled roof or clerestory daylighting to reduce daytime lighting energy in deep-plan warehouse areas without introducing large thermal penalties.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: strong daytime lighting reduction where top-lighting reaches large floor areas.
- **End-Uses Affected**: interior lighting and some cooling.

## Basis / References
- The warehouse guide explicitly recommends skylights and north-facing clerestories with daylight-responsive controls.
- Daylighting is especially valuable in deep-plan storage buildings.

## Assumptions
Requires suitable roof geometry and lighting controls; best in regularly occupied daylit zones.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones with climate-appropriate skylight thermal performance.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with lower LPD design and high-reflectance interior surfaces.

## Implementation Essentials
- Use top-lighting where deep-plan areas cannot benefit from façade daylight.
- Favor north-oriented daylight where clerestories are used.
- Keep skylight area disciplined and pair with dimming or switching controls.
- Coordinate waterproofing, structure, and maintenance access.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poorly selected skylights can increase heat gain/loss or leakage risk.
- Warehouses with limited daytime occupancy may see reduced value.

## KPIs
- Daytime lighting kWh
- Daylight-zone hours at reduced output
- Maintenance incidents related to roof apertures
- User visibility/safety feedback

## M&V Plan
- Compare daytime lighting energy pre/post or against similar non-daylit zones.
- Review daylight-control operation seasonally.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate; strongest in continuously used daytime warehouse areas.

# ECM: Roof, Wall, and Door Envelope Upgrade for Conditioned Warehouses

## Summary
Improve roof, wall, and door thermal performance and air sealing in conditioned warehouse or self-storage buildings to reduce heating/cooling loads and improve comfort near openings.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: envelope is a core recommendation area in the warehouse guide.
- **Typical effect**: strongest in conditioned facilities and in climates with large delta-T.
- **End-Uses Affected**: heating, cooling, and perimeter comfort.

## Basis / References
- The warehouse guide includes climate-zone recommendations for roofs, walls, slabs, doors, glazing, and skylights.
- Load reduction is emphasized before HVAC sizing.

## Assumptions
Most valuable in conditioned warehouses, not unconditioned storage.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones; strongest in cold and hot-humid climates.

## Interaction Notes
Supports smaller HVAC sizing, lower infiltration, and more stable loading dock/perimeter conditions.

## Implementation Essentials
- Prioritize roof and door leakage/thermal issues first, then walls and glazing.
- Improve weatherstripping and sealing at overhead and personnel doors.
- Revisit HVAC loads after envelope changes.
- Combine with planned maintenance or reroofing where possible.

## Risks / Constraints
- Infiltration at doors can dominate if only nominal insulation is improved.
- Some self-storage applications have limited value if spaces are largely unconditioned.

## KPIs
- Heating and cooling EUI
- Perimeter comfort complaints
- Identified leakage defects resolved
- HVAC peak demand

## M&V Plan
- Compare seasonal HVAC energy pre/post with weather normalization.
- Use spot QA or IR checks for key defect areas.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Variable; strongest when coordinated with maintenance cycles.

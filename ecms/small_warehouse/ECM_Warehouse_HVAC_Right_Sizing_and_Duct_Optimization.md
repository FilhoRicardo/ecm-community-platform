# ECM: HVAC Right-Sizing with Interior Duct Routing and Low-Static Design

## Summary
Right-size packaged HVAC for actual warehouse loads and reduce distribution losses by keeping ducts inside the conditioned space, sealing them well, and designing for low friction.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: meaningful reduction in fan and conditioning energy where baseline duct design is wasteful or equipment is oversized.
- **End-Uses Affected**: heating, cooling, and fan energy.

## Basis / References
- The warehouse guide explicitly recommends proper load calculations, no ductwork outside conditioned space, duct insulation, duct sealing, and low friction-rate design.
- Oversizing is called out as a problem in the integrated-strategy tables.

## Assumptions
Most useful in conditioned warehouses with meaningful air distribution rather than simple direct-fired or minimally conditioned spaces.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Depends on lighting and envelope load reductions being reflected in final equipment sizing.

## Implementation Essentials
- Recalculate loads after envelope and lighting improvements.
- Route ducts inside the conditioned enclosure wherever possible.
- Seal all joints and minimize unnecessary fittings/pressure losses.
- Select fans and motors to match the lower-static design.

## Risks / Constraints
- Structural and layout constraints may tempt designers into exterior duct runs.
- Oversizing can persist if default rules of thumb override actual calculations.

## KPIs
- HVAC kWh
- Fan energy
- Static pressure
- Duct leakage findings

## M&V Plan
- Compare HVAC energy and fan trends before/after.
- Use TAB results and pressure trends as supporting evidence.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate in new construction; retrofit value depends on duct accessibility.

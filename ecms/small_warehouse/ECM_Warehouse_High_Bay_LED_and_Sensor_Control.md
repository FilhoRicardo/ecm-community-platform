# ECM: High-Bay LED Lighting with Occupancy and Daylight Controls

## Summary
Use efficient high-bay lighting, aisle-appropriate zoning, occupancy controls, and daylight response so warehouses are not lit to full output when areas are vacant or daylit.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: lighting is one of the strongest warehouse end-use opportunities.
- **Typical effect**: major electricity reduction where older HID or uncontrolled fluorescent systems are present.
- **End-Uses Affected**: warehouse lighting and secondary cooling.

## Basis / References
- The warehouse guide emphasizes reduced interior lighting power, efficient luminaires, daylighting, and occupancy controls.
- Warehouse lighting must match task type: bulk storage, fine storage, and office/support spaces differ.

## Assumptions
Best where occupancy is intermittent and daylighting is usable.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with skylights/clerestories, high-reflectance ceilings, and task-based zoning.

## Implementation Essentials
- Replace high-bay fixtures with efficient optical distributions suited to aisle/rack geometry.
- Zone by storage/task type rather than one broad warehouse circuit.
- Use occupancy controls in aisles and low-use storage where traffic patterns allow.
- Calibrate daylight controls separately in top-lit zones.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor sensor placement in aisles can create nuisance switching.
- Overaggressive dimming may concern safety or picking accuracy if levels are not validated.

## KPIs
- Lighting kWh
- Occupied vs unoccupied runtime
- Daylight-zone dimming fraction
- Safety/visibility complaints

## M&V Plan
- Compare lighting panel energy and runtime pre/post.
- Use control logs to verify occupancy and daylight response.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Usually favorable, especially in older high-bay facilities.

# ECM: Kitchen Exhaust and Makeup-Air Integration

## Summary
Integrate kitchen exhaust, makeup air, and store pressurization so prepared-food operations do not pull humid air through the building or create unnecessary HVAC penalties.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: meaningful HVAC and fan savings plus better humidity control where kitchens are active.
- **End-Uses Affected**: kitchen exhaust fan energy, makeup-air conditioning, and store latent load.

## Basis / References
- The grocery guide explicitly discusses kitchen/HVAC interaction and makeup-air balance.
- Prepared-food areas can strongly affect whole-store pressure and humidity.

## Assumptions
Most valuable in grocery stores with significant prepared-food/kitchen operations.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones, especially hot-humid climates.

## Interaction Notes
Strongly coupled to store positive pressure, DOAS strategy, and kitchen DCV.

## Implementation Essentials
- Quantify exhaust requirements and actual cooking profiles.
- Provide makeup air deliberately rather than relying on store infiltration.
- Coordinate transfer air, DOAS, and kitchen cooling needs.
- Verify whole-store pressure after changes.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor makeup-air design can create humidity problems at the front of house.
- Kitchen staff may bypass controls if comfort suffers.

## KPIs
- Kitchen hood fan runtime
- Store pressure relationship
- RH/dew point
- Makeup-air unit energy

## M&V Plan
- Trend fan speeds, pressure, and humidity pre/post.
- Compare kitchen-associated HVAC energy after normalization.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate; strongest where kitchens are active and currently unmanaged.

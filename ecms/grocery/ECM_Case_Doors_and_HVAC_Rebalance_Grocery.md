# ECM: Refrigerated Case Doors with HVAC and Humidity Rebalancing

## Summary
Add glass doors to refrigerated cases and then rebalance HVAC and latent-control strategy so refrigeration savings are captured without creating new humidity or comfort problems.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: high-impact grocery ECM because case infiltration is a major refrigeration load.
- **Typical effect**: large refrigeration reduction plus changed dehumidification and heating balance.
- **End-Uses Affected**: refrigeration electricity, HVAC latent load, and store comfort.

## Basis / References
- The grocery guide explicitly notes that case doors materially reduce infiltration and change HVAC/dehumidification behavior.
- This is one of the clearest system-interaction measures in the guide.

## Assumptions
Best where medium- and low-temp open cases dominate and customer/merchandising goals still allow doors.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones, especially hot-humid climates where HVAC latent impact is significant.

## Interaction Notes
This is not just a refrigeration measure; it changes store balance point, humidity control, and sometimes heating demand.

## Implementation Essentials
- Select case types where doors are commercially acceptable.
- Recalculate HVAC sensible/latent assumptions after door adoption.
- Recommission humidity control and positive-pressure strategy.
- Review anti-sweat, visibility, and customer access performance.

## Risks / Constraints
- If HVAC is not rebalanced, comfort or humidity problems may follow.
- Merchandising teams may resist door deployment in some categories.

## KPIs
- Refrigeration kWh
- Store RH/dew point
- Anti-sweat heater runtime
- Comfort and merchandising complaints

## M&V Plan
- Compare refrigeration energy and store humidity before/after.
- Trend HVAC latent-control behavior after door installation.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Often strong where open-case infiltration is high, but HVAC rebalancing must be included.

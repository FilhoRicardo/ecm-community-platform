# ECM: LED School Lighting with Occupancy and Daylight Controls

## Summary
Use high-efficacy lighting with room-appropriate occupancy, schedule, and daylight controls so classrooms, admin, and support spaces are not overlit or left on unnecessarily.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: core zero-energy K-12 strategy.
- **Typical effect**: major whole-building electricity reduction when legacy fluorescent systems or uncontrolled schedules are replaced.
- **End-Uses Affected**: interior lighting and secondary cooling.

## Basis / References
- The zero-energy K-12 guide organizes electric-lighting and control strategies by school space type.
- It treats controls as integral to realized savings.

## Assumptions
Requires room-type-specific control logic rather than a single school-wide default.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with daylighting, task-specific lighting, and occupant engagement.

## Implementation Essentials
- Use lower LPD targets by classroom, admin, corridor, library, and support space.
- Apply vacancy/occupancy control in eligible rooms and schedule logic after hours.
- Calibrate daylight controls in sidelighted/toplit zones.
- Recommission after furniture/AV changes.

## Risks / Constraints
- Nuisance switching or poor dimming can create teacher resistance.
- Overly generic district standards may undermine space-specific optimization.

## KPIs
- Lighting kWh by area type
- After-hours lighting runtime
- Override rates
- Teacher/staff satisfaction

## M&V Plan
- Compare panel or wing-level lighting energy and runtime pre/post.
- Use control trend logs for persistent verification.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Usually favorable, especially when relighting older schools.

# ECM: DOAS and IAQ-Optimized School Ventilation

## Summary
Use dedicated outdoor air systems and school-specific control logic so indoor air quality remains strong without overventilating or overconditioning the building.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: core HVAC pathway for zero-energy schools.
- **Typical effect**: material heating/cooling and fan savings when ventilation is decoupled and delivered accurately.
- **End-Uses Affected**: ventilation heating/cooling, fan energy, and humidity control.

## Basis / References
- The zero-energy K-12 guide includes DOAS as a dedicated HVAC strategy and repeatedly links IAQ with the energy goal.
- Healthy learning conditions are a first-order design requirement.

## Assumptions
Requires accurate ventilation design, balancing, and controls discipline.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones, especially humid and extreme climates.

## Interaction Notes
Works with GSHP/VRF/system-path choices, classroom schedules, and occupancy patterns.

## Implementation Essentials
- Deliver required outdoor air accurately to each school zone.
- Control humidity and pressure explicitly, not implicitly.
- Avoid inflated OA assumptions where standards do not require them.
- Trend CO₂, humidity, and ventilation runtime after occupancy.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor balancing or zoning can create IAQ complaints quickly.
- Operators may disable advanced sequences if the system is not understandable.

## KPIs
- CO₂ and humidity trends
- Ventilation system energy
- IAQ complaints
- Fan runtime and outside-air delivery rate

## M&V Plan
- Use BAS trend review for CO₂, humidity, and airflow where available.
- Compare ventilation-related energy against modeled expectations.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate to high depending on system architecture, but central to zero-energy school strategy.

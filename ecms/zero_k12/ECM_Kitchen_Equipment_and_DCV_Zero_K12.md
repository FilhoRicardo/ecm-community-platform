# ECM: School Kitchen Equipment Upgrade with Demand-Controlled Ventilation

## Summary
Upgrade cafeteria kitchen equipment and use demand-controlled hood ventilation so food-service loads are reduced without compromising throughput or safety.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: meaningful process and HVAC savings in schools with substantial food-service operations.
- **End-Uses Affected**: kitchen process energy, exhaust fan energy, and makeup-air conditioning.

## Basis / References
- The zero-energy K-12 guide has dedicated sections for kitchen equipment and school-specific process loads.
- Kitchen systems are one of the school-specific differences from office/K-12 classroom-only strategies.

## Assumptions
Applies best where kitchens are more than simple reheating operations.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Strong interaction with ventilation, makeup air, and service water heating.

## Implementation Essentials
- Match kitchen equipment count and size to actual meal program demand.
- Use hood controls based on cooking activity.
- Coordinate makeup air and space pressure with adjacent areas.
- Review operating schedules and cleaning practices with food-service staff.

## Risks / Constraints
- If kitchen throughput is underestimated, staff may reject equipment or hood setpoints.
- Controls must preserve capture and safety performance.

## KPIs
- Kitchen kWh and gas
- Hood fan runtime and speed profile
- Makeup-air conditioning energy
- Staff complaints about kitchen comfort/performance

## M&V Plan
- Measure kitchen panel/fan energy and compare before/after.
- Review hood control trends during representative meal periods.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate, strongest in full-service school kitchens.

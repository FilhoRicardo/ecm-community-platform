# ECM: Toplighting for Gyms and Multipurpose Spaces

## Summary
Use toplighting in large-volume school spaces so daytime lighting energy is reduced without creating glare or maintenance-heavy roof conditions.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: strong reduction in daytime electric lighting in gyms and multipurpose rooms.
- **End-Uses Affected**: lighting and some secondary cooling.

## Basis / References
- The zero-energy K-12 guide includes dedicated gym/multipurpose toplighting strategies.
- These large-volume spaces are among the best candidates for controlled toplighting.

## Assumptions
Works best where school hours align with daylight and roof design can accommodate maintenance-safe apertures.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones with good thermal control of toplighting assemblies.

## Interaction Notes
Depends on daylight harvesting and skylight/roof monitor thermal performance.

## Implementation Essentials
- Use diffusing toplighting devices rather than uncontrolled direct-beam openings.
- Pair with dimming controls.
- Coordinate sports, assemblies, AV blackout needs, and maintenance access.
- Review roof insulation and air sealing around apertures.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor diffusion can create glare or hot spots.
- Roof leakage and maintenance concerns can reduce owner acceptance.

## KPIs
- Daytime lighting kWh
- Gym lighting runtime at full output
- User complaints about glare
- Toplighting-related maintenance events

## M&V Plan
- Compare daytime lighting use before/after or against similar non-toplit spaces.
- Verify control dimming fraction and occupant acceptance.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate to high depending on roof strategy; strongest in large-volume spaces.

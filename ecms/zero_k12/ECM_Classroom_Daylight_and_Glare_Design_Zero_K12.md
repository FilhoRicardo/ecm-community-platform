# ECM: Classroom Daylighting with Glare-Safe Teaching-Wall Design

## Summary
Design classroom sidelighting/toplighting so daylight is useful for learning and AV use, not something teachers immediately block with blinds.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: major electricity reduction pathway in zero-energy K-12 design.
- **Typical effect**: strong lighting savings when daylight remains usable during most school hours.
- **End-Uses Affected**: classroom lighting and secondary cooling.

## Basis / References
- The zero-energy K-12 guide has dedicated classroom sidelighting, combined sidelighting/toplighting, and daylight/visual-comfort content.
- The guide also stresses classroom usability, not daylight quantity alone.

## Assumptions
Requires classroom layout, teaching wall, and control zoning to be coordinated from early design.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with daylight harvesting, shading, interior reflectance, and AV compatibility.

## Implementation Essentials
- Coordinate glazing, teaching-wall position, projector/display needs, and desk orientation.
- Use exterior shading and diffusion to control direct sun.
- Keep daylight control zones separate from teaching-display zones.
- Mock up representative classrooms where possible.

## Risks / Constraints
- Good daylight design can fail if AV and blinds are not planned together.
- Teachers may permanently defeat controls if glare persists.

## KPIs
- Classroom lighting kWh
- Blind closure rates
- Daylight autonomy in learning zones
- Teacher satisfaction / glare complaints

## M&V Plan
- Use classroom daylight simulation in design.
- Post-occupancy, compare classroom lighting runtime and control behavior by façade type.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate planning/design effort with high operational value.

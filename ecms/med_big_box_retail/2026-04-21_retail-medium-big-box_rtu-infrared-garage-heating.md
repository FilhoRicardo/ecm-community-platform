# ECM: RTU Infrared Heater Garage Heating

## Summary

**What:** Replace conventional forced-air unit heaters in loading docks and stockrooms with infrared radiant heaters — heat the space and objects directly rather than conditioning the entire air volume.

**Why:** Loading docks and stockrooms in big-box retail are typically unconditioned or poorly conditioned with high infiltration. Installing radiant heaters over active loading areas warms workers and concrete directly, eliminating the massive losses from heating large air volumes. DOE Gas Infrared Heating Guide and HPAC Engineering field data document 30–50% heating energy reduction in similar applications.

## Estimated Savings
- **30–50% garage/dock heating energy**
- **20–40 kBtu/sq ft/yr**

## Basis / References
- DOE Gas Infrared Heating Guide — federal technical reference for sizing and application of gas-fired infrared heating systems
- HPAC Engineering, "Radiant Heating Retrofits," 2018 — practitioner field study documenting 30–50% garage heating energy reduction in commercial dock applications

## Assumptions
- Ceiling height ≥ 14 ft in dock area (infrared requires clear sightline — emitters must be above any obstructions)
- Dock is open ≤ 40% of operating hours (otherwise full building conditioning may be more efficient)
- Gas supply available at adequate pressure (natural gas 5–7" WC)
- Loading dock is actively used for ≥ 4 hours/day (zoning justifies added controls)
- Structural support exists for radiant heater mounting

## Implementation Essentials

1. **Survey loading dock areas** — record ceiling height, clearances, and obstructions; note areas of highest worker activity (loading positions, forklift staging)
2. **Size radiant heaters** based on area coverage (typically 8–12 ft throw radius per heater) and desired comfort temperature (typically 55°F at dock level)
3. **Specify ceramic or tube-style infrared burners** — ceramic for spot heating per bay; tube-style for continuous linear dock coverage
4. **Install with proper clearances** per manufacturer — typically 12–18" from ceiling, 7 ft minimum clearance from combustible materials
5. **Zone controls** — install individual unit ON/OFF or staged thermostats per bay or per 20 ft section; heat only actively-used dock positions
6. **Integrate with existing gas line** — verify gas pressure and sizing; install shut-off valve per code
7. **Commission and test** — verify infrared pattern coverage with thermal imaging

## Risks / Constraints
- **Fire code clearance**: maintain minimum clearances from combustibles — document in O&M manual
- **Ceiling height minimum**: infrared effectiveness drops significantly below 12 ft; do not specify if ceiling < 14 ft without re-sizing
- **Explosive atmosphere**: if dock stores flammable materials, use explosion-proof radiant heaters or exclude those zones
- **Worker acclimation**: dock workers may prefer forced-air for full-body warmth; involve facilities management and dock workers in pilot decision
- **Do not remove existing unit heaters entirely** — retain as backup or for summer stockroom conditioning

## KPIs
- Natural Gas usage (therms/HDD by zone) — submetered
- Dock worker comfort complaints (count per month)
- Heater runtime hours per zone (from gas valve signal or hour meter)
- Loading dock air temperature at worker height (°F)

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)

- Install dedicated natural gas submeter on dock heating zone (or use existing bill reconciliation)
- Baseline: 12-month gas usage normalized to HDD for dock area
- Post-implementation: 12-month gas usage (longer baseline due to weather normalization requirements for gas)
- IPMVP Option C: whole-dock natural gas measurement with weather adjustment

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Infrared heater units: $800–1,500/unit × ~8 units (for 8-bay dock) = $6,400–12,000
- Gas piping, zoning, and installation labor: $4,000–8,000
- Controls and thermostats: $1,500–3,000
- Total: $12,000–23,000
- **Payback: 2–5 years** (20–40 kBtu/sq ft/yr savings × 20,000 sq ft dock × $0.60–0.90/kBtu gas cost)

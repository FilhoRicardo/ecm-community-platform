# ECM: Selective Retail Daylighting with Controls and Merchandise Protection

## Summary
Use daylight only where it supports the customer experience and does not create glare, product fade, or uncontrolled contrast on the sales floor.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: useful lighting reduction in selected zones, especially large-floorplate stores with toplighting.
- **End-Uses Affected**: lighting and some cooling.

## Basis / References
- The retail AEDG includes general daylighting strategy, skylight integration, and successful-retail-daylighting examples.
- It treats daylight as selective and controlled, not universally beneficial in every aisle.

## Assumptions
Best for entry zones, circulation, and selected sales areas where merchandise is not glare/fade sensitive.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones, with stronger solar-control needs in hot and mixed climates.

## Interaction Notes
Depends on daylight harvesting, glazing/skylight thermal quality, and merchandising strategy.

## Implementation Essentials
- Target daylight to appropriate sales and circulation zones rather than the entire floor blindly.
- Use diffusing toplighting or carefully located sidelighting.
- Keep photosensor zones aligned with display layout and future resets.
- Review product sensitivity and visual contrast before expanding daylight.

## Risks / Constraints
- Uncontrolled daylight can damage merchandising outcomes or create glare at checkout.
- Frequent display resets can undermine control zoning.

## KPIs
- Daylit-zone lighting kWh
- Blind/blackout use in daylit areas
- Visual comfort complaints
- Sales-floor daylight control overrides

## M&V Plan
- Compare lighting runtime and dimming fraction in daylit zones.
- Observe merchandising performance and override behavior seasonally.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate; strongest in large-floorplate stores where toplighting is practical.

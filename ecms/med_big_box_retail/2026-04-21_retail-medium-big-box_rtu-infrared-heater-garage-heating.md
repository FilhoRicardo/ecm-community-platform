# ECM: RTU Infrared Heater Garage Heating

## Summary

**What:** Replace conventional forced-air unit heaters in loading docks and stockrooms with infrared radiant heaters — heat the space and objects directly rather than conditioning the entire air volume.

**Why:** Loading docks and stockrooms in big-box retail are typically unconditioned or poorly conditioned. Installing radiant heaters over loading areas warms workers and concrete directly, eliminating the massive losses from heating large air volumes with high infiltration.

## Estimated Savings
- **30–50% garage/dock heating energy** HVAC energy
- **20–40 kBtu/sq ft/yr**

## Basis / References
- DOE Gas Infrared Heating Guide
- HPAC Engineering 'Radiant Heating Retrofits' 2018

## Assumptions
- Building operates ≥ 8 hr/day on weekdays
- BAS is programmable with CO₂ sensor integration
- ASHRAE 62.1 compliance verified — CO₂ differential method acceptable for occupancy-based demand control ventilation
- For RTU infrared: dock area has ≥ 16 ft ceiling height for proper infrared coverage

## Implementation Essentials
1. Survey loading dock areas — note ceiling height (infrared requires clear sightline).
2. Size radiant heaters based on area coverage and desired comfort temperature.
3. Install ceramic or tube infrared burners.
4. Zone control — heat only actively-used dock positions.

## Risks / Constraints
- ASHRAE 62.1 minimum OA rates must be maintained at all times — CO₂ differential method requires baseline occupancy signal
- Code compliance: verify local amendment to ASHRAE 62.1-2019 §6.2.7 for OA reset allowance
- For infrared: clear sightline required from heater to target surface — no obstructions between emitter and floor/worker zone
- Infrared combustion products (CO, NO₂) must be vented adequately in enclosed docks

## KPIs
- Natural Gas usage (therms/HDD by zone)
- Dock worker complaints
- Heater runtime hours

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)
- Install whole-building gas and electric submeters on affected RTU/AHU circuits
- Baseline period: 12 months pre-installation; reporting period: 6 months post-installation
- Normalize savings to HDD/climate conditions and occupancy using linear regression
- For VAV: monitor fan kW trend via BAS; for AHU OAT reset: track OA damper % and heating valve position

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- **Payback: 2–5 years**
- CO₂ sensor installation: $200–$400/sensor; typical office: 4–8 sensors
- BAS programming: $2,000–$5,000
- Infrared heater installation: $3,000–$8,000 per dock bay
- Total project range: $5,000–$20,000 for office; $15,000–$50,000 for big-box dock retrofit

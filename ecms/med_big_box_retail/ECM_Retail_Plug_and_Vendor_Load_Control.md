# ECM: Vendor-Supplied Plug Load and POS Equipment Control

## Summary
Reduce miscellaneous retail loads from kiosks, POS equipment, displays, and vendor-supplied devices through procurement standards and after-hours control.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: moderate but persistent electricity reduction, especially in after-hours baseload.
- **End-Uses Affected**: plug electricity and cooling from internal gains.

## Basis / References
- The retail AEDG includes plug-load guidance and recognizes vendor-supplied equipment as a recurring opportunity.
- POS and display equipment often escapes formal energy review.

## Assumptions
Most useful where many distributed devices run continuously or lack native sleep/standby logic.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Supports prototype standardization and lower after-hours HVAC load.

## Implementation Essentials
- Inventory kiosks, POS, displays, chargers, and vendor devices by store type.
- Require standby or timed shutoff features in procurement.
- Separate critical always-on devices from controllable loads.
- Review after-hours baseload monthly.

## Risks / Constraints
- Vendors may resist control requirements.
- Store operations may bypass shutdowns for convenience.

## KPIs
- After-hours plug baseload
- Controlled vs uncontrolled plug load
- Device compliance with procurement standard
- Whole-store nighttime kW

## M&V Plan
- Compare nighttime baseload before/after control rollout.
- Sample representative device loads where panel metering is unavailable.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Low to moderate; strongest when combined with procurement refresh cycles.

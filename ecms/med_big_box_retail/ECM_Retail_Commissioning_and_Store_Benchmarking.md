# ECM: Retail Commissioning, Benchmarking, and Prototype Feedback

## Summary
Use commissioning and normalized store benchmarking so proven measures are retained in operation and weak-performing stores are identified quickly.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical effect**: protects HVAC, lighting, and control ECMs and helps scale good practice across stores.
- **End-Uses Affected**: whole-building operations indirectly.

## Basis / References
- The retail AEDG includes quality assurance, M&V, and benchmarking in the project process and implementation chapters.
- Portfolio learning is central to medium/big-box retail energy success.

## Assumptions
Requires utility data discipline and willingness to compare stores objectively.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Supports prototype evolution, lighting schedules, humidity-control persistence, and equipment standardization.

## Implementation Essentials
- Commission key systems and verify store schedules before opening.
- Benchmark stores by climate, hours, and merchandising intensity.
- Investigate outliers instead of normalizing poor performance.
- Feed lessons into the next prototype revision.

## Risks / Constraints
- Benchmarking without climate/operational normalization can mislead.
- Store teams may override controls without visibility.

## KPIs
- EUI by store and climate
- Schedule compliance
- Outlier store count
- Commissioning issue closure rate

## M&V Plan
- Use whole-building utility benchmarking plus targeted subsystem trend review.
- Track persistence through monthly exception reporting.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Low soft-cost measure with strong portfolio value.


## Templates / Reuse
- ECM screening worksheet
- Climate-zone applicability note
- KPI / M&V checklist

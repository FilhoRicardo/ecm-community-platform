# ECM: Sales Floor Lighting Redesign with Lower LPD and Better Accent Strategy

## Summary
Reduce sales-floor lighting power by replacing blanket high ambient levels with layered ambient, display, and selective accent lighting that still supports merchandising.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: lighting is one of the strongest retail electricity reduction opportunities.
- **Typical effect**: major lighting savings where stores rely on older high-wattage ambient and poorly targeted accent lighting.
- **End-Uses Affected**: interior lighting and secondary cooling.

## Basis / References
- The retail AEDG includes goals for retail lighting, sample design layouts, and specific attention to accent and perimeter display lighting.
- The guide recognizes that merchandising quality must be maintained, not ignored.

## Assumptions
Works best where the sales floor is currently overlit or accent lighting has spread beyond targeted product zones.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with daylighting, scheduling, and LED source selection. Reduces cooling load slightly.

## Implementation Essentials
- Audit current ambient, display, and accent layers separately.
- Lower baseline ambient levels and reserve higher output for true feature areas.
- Use LED optics that create contrast and product visibility without brute-force wattage.
- Validate revised scenes with merchandising and store operations teams.

## Risks / Constraints
- Merchandising teams may resist if visual hierarchy is not preserved.
- Poor optic selection can reduce perceived product quality.

## KPIs
- Sales-floor LPD
- Lighting kWh
- Accent-light runtime
- Merchandising/staff feedback

## M&V Plan
- Compare lighting panel energy and runtime pre/post redesign.
- Review visual merchandising acceptance after rollout.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate; often attractive in relight cycles or prototype refreshes.

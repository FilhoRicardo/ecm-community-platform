# ECM: LED Surgical and Clinical Lighting Optimization

## Summary
Upgrade surgical, patient-care, and support lighting to high-efficacy LED with scene control so lighting energy and cooling loads fall without compromising clinical function.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: the guide specifically highlights LED surgical task lighting and lower whole-building lighting power.
- **Typical effect**: strong electricity reduction plus lower heat gain in clinical spaces.
- **End-Uses Affected**: interior lighting and secondary cooling.

## Basis / References
- The large-hospital guide explicitly cites LED surgery lighting as a major opportunity.
- It also emphasizes lighting quality and quiet-at-night capability.

## Assumptions
Works best where existing systems are high wattage or poor at dimming/scene control.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with quiet-at-night scenes, daylighting, and better clinical task layering.

## Implementation Essentials
- Separate ambient, exam, task, observation, and night-light functions.
- Use high-quality clinical LEDs with appropriate color performance and cleanability.
- Commission scenes with end users rather than relying on default settings.
- Review cooling implications in surgical and high-intensity zones.

## Risks / Constraints
- Clinical acceptance depends on visual quality, not energy metrics alone.
- Poor usability can cause permanent overrides.

## KPIs
- Lighting kWh
- Scene runtime
- Clinical staff feedback
- Cooling load in relit areas

## M&V Plan
- Compare panel or departmental lighting energy before/after.
- Review scene usage and user feedback after stabilization.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate; often best when tied to clinical refurbishment cycles.

# ECM: Air-Side Heat Recovery for Large-Hospital Ventilation Streams

## Summary
Recover sensible or total energy from large hospital exhaust streams to reduce the cost of conditioning outdoor air.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: major recommendation in the large-hospital guide.
- **Typical effect**: strong where outdoor-air and exhaust volumes are high and continuous.
- **End-Uses Affected**: ventilation heating/cooling and reheat burden.

## Basis / References
- The large-hospital guide includes Air-Side Heat Recovery as a dedicated chapter element and executive-summary measure.
- Hospitals are especially well suited because ventilation rates are large and consistent.

## Assumptions
Requires acceptable stream pairing, contamination review, and maintenance capability.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones, strongest in extreme and humid climates.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with decoupled OA treatment, pressure control, and plant heat-recovery strategies.

## Implementation Essentials
- Select recovery technology compatible with hospital exhaust and code constraints.
- Manage frost, bypass, and maintenance access from the start.
- Limit pressure-drop penalties through good air-side design.
- Trend effectiveness and bypass behavior in operation.

## Risks / Constraints
- Clinical contamination concerns may limit technology choice.
- Pressure-drop and maintenance neglect can erode expected value.

## KPIs
- Recovery effectiveness
- OA treatment energy
- Pressure drop
- Humidity and temperature control stability

## M&V Plan
- Use trend-derived heat-recovery calculations and subsystem energy data where available.
- Compare OA treatment energy before/after or against modeled design case.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate to high; usually justified when ventilation loads are very large.

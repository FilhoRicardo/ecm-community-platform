# ECM: Program Critical vs Noncritical Areas Before HVAC Escalation

## Summary
Classify spaces accurately so the most energy-intensive ventilation, pressure, and thermal-control strategies are reserved for truly critical areas.

## Estimated Savings
- **AEDG basis**: foundational hospital strategy because overclassification drives permanent fan, reheat, and plant energy.
- **Typical effect**: major design-stage reduction in unnecessary air-side intensity.
- **End-Uses Affected**: ventilation, fan energy, reheat, and plant load.

## Basis / References
- The large-hospital AEDG includes a dedicated critical vs noncritical space-planning section.
- Clinical function and patient care are treated as first-order constraints.

## Assumptions
Best applied very early, before system architecture and room standards are frozen.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Shapes whether advanced VAV, decoupled OA, and setback strategies are available.

## Implementation Essentials
- Review each department and room type against actual clinical need and governing standards.
- Reserve highest air-change and pressure requirements only where justified.
- Separate support/admin/public spaces for lower-energy approaches.
- Revisit classifications when department planning changes.

## Risks / Constraints
- Overclassification is common and difficult to undo later.
- Underclassification creates compliance and patient-safety risk.

## KPIs
- Area by criticality category
- Airflow intensity by department
- Reheat intensity
- Number of spaces using high-intensity control without true need

## M&V Plan
- Design-stage verification through room data sheets and airflow budgets.
- Post-occupancy, compare actual airflows against classified requirements.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Low direct capex, very high design leverage.

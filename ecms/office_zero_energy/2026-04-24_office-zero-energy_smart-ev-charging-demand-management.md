# ECM: Smart EV Charging / Demand Management

## Summary

**What:** Install smart EV charge management software (e.g. Monta, Ohme, Fuuse) to schedule charging during off-peak periods and prevent demand spikes, particularly at sites with half-hourly meter demand charges.

**Why:** At XYZ Building, Radisson Blu Stansted, and Akzo Nobel HQ, EV charging is growing. Without demand management, simultaneous vehicle charging creates demand spikes that drive significant demand charges.

## Estimated Savings
- 5–15% peak demand cost reduction (not energy volume)
- £500–5,000/yr avoided demand charges

## Basis / References
- Portfolio basis: XYZ Building, Radisson Blu Stansted, Akzo Nobel HQ

## Assumptions
- Building-specific survey required before implementation
- Energy audit recommended to quantify baseline and savings

## Implementation Essentials
1. 1) Audit current EV charging infrastructure — number of points, max load per point. 2) Install smart charge controllers with demand management capability. 3) Configure off-peak charging schedule (typically 11pm–7am). 4) Set demand limit per transformer or main incomer. 5) Integrate with building EMS for monitoring.

## Risks / Constraints
- ⚠️ Savings are demand cost, not energy volume. If on flat-rate tariff, savings are minimal. Essential infrastructure risk management as EV fleet grows.

## KPIs
- EV charging demand (kW)
- Peak demand (kW)
- Demand charge (£/month)
- Charging events (#/day)

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)
- Pre-installation baseline: 90-day continuous monitoring
- Post-installation: compare same period following year (weather-normalised)
- IPMVP Option C: Whole-building metering with regression analysis

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Payback: 6–18 months
- Cost: Requires building-specific survey and contractor pricing

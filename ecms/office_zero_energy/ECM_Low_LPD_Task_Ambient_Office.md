# ECM: Low-LPD Task/Ambient Office Lighting Design

## Summary
Reduce office ambient lighting power density and use layered, task-appropriate lighting rather than blanket overlighting.

## Estimated Savings
- **ASHRAE AEDG basis**: lower connected load and lower annual lighting EUI as a core design strategy.
- **Typical effect**: meaningful reduction compared with traditional broad ambient office lighting.
- **End-Uses Affected**: lighting electricity and sensible cooling.

## Basis / References
- AEDGZE Small to Medium Office, Chapter 5 Electric Lighting design strategies and space-specific strategies.

## Assumptions
Works best when space planning, workstation type, and control philosophy are coordinated.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs with daylighting, occupancy controls, and high-reflectance finishes.

## Implementation Essentials
- Set target ambient illuminance based on actual tasks and screen use.
- Use task lighting selectively where needed instead of raising whole-zone ambient levels.
- Review vertical illumination, screen contrast, and glare as part of design.
- Coordinate with tenant expectations before procurement.

## Risks / Constraints
- Occupants may perceive lower ambient targets as poor lighting if design intent is not explained.
- Task lighting can be added ad hoc if workstation needs were not understood.

## KPIs
- Installed LPD
- Lighting kWh per m²
- Task-light deployment rate post-occupancy
- Visual comfort survey results

## M&V Plan
- Verify installed power against design intent.
- Use lighting panel or floor-level energy comparison plus occupant feedback after stabilization.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Often cost-neutral or better when fixture count is reduced intelligently.

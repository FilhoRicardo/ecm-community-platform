# ECM: Air-to-Air Energy Recovery on Office Outdoor Air Systems

## Summary
Recover sensible or total energy from exhaust air streams to reduce the heating and cooling cost of office ventilation air.

## Estimated Savings
- **ASHRAE AEDG basis**: ventilation-load reduction with strong effect where OA energy is material.
- **Typical effect**: most valuable in climates with significant enthalpy difference between outdoor and exhaust air.
- **End-Uses Affected**: ventilation heating/cooling and reheat burden.

## Basis / References
- AEDGZE Small to Medium Office includes a sidebar on Air-to-Air Series Energy Recovery and DOAS-focused strategies.

## Assumptions
Requires suitable exhaust/supply stream arrangement, acceptable cross-contamination risk profile, and manageable pressure drop.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones, with strongest value in very hot, very cold, or humid climates.

## Interaction Notes
Supports DOAS optimization and can materially reduce reheating or latent-treatment burden.

## Implementation Essentials
- Select recovery device type based on exhaust cleanliness, latent need, pressure drop, and maintenance strategy.
- Review bypass, frost, and shoulder-season control logic carefully.
- Coordinate exhaust-system routing early enough to keep duct paths practical.
- Trend effectiveness and bypass position during operation.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor maintenance can degrade wheel/core performance quickly.
- Pressure-drop penalties can offset value if fan systems are not designed carefully.

## KPIs
- Recovery effectiveness
- Ventilation heating/cooling energy
- Bypass hours
- Pressure drop across recovery device

## M&V Plan
- Use DOAS and plant trend data to compare modeled versus actual treatment energy.
- Verify effectiveness with temperature/humidity trend snapshots under representative conditions.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate capital measure; strongest justification where outdoor-air loads are high and runtime is long.

# ECM: Air-Source VRF Heat Pump with DOAS

## Summary
Use air-source VRF with dedicated outdoor air to decouple ventilation from sensible conditioning and support low-energy office operation.

## Estimated Savings
- **ASHRAE AEDG basis**: one of the guide’s principal office HVAC pathways.
- **Typical effect**: strongest where zoning flexibility, part-load performance, and electrification are priorities.
- **End-Uses Affected**: whole HVAC electricity and ventilation conditioning.

## Basis / References
- AEDGZE Small to Medium Office includes System B—Air-Source VRF Heat Pump with DOAS.

## Assumptions
Requires careful attention to cold-climate heating capability, defrost behavior, zoning count, and DOAS integration.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones; strongest in moderate and mixed climates, with more scrutiny needed for cold-climate heating performance.

## Interaction Notes
Works best with strong envelope performance, good control integration, and a DOAS that handles ventilation and humidity well.

## Implementation Essentials
- Keep the DOAS role explicit rather than letting zone units shoulder latent and ventilation loads.
- Check heating performance at design winter conditions and during defrost.
- Coordinate refrigerant piping, condensate, and maintenance access early.
- Trend simultaneous heating/cooling behavior and override patterns after occupancy.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor DOAS integration can create humidity and ventilation problems.
- Maintenance quality materially affects long-term performance.
- Cold-climate performance assumptions are often optimistic.

## KPIs
- Whole HVAC kWh
- DOAS energy use
- Zone comfort/override counts
- Heating performance during low ambient conditions

## M&V Plan
- Prefer subsystem metering for DOAS and VRF where possible.
- Use BAS trends for zone temperatures, DOAS operation, and seasonal performance.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Project-specific; stronger value where electrification and zoning flexibility are explicit goals.

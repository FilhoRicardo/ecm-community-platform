# ECM: Computer and Monitor Power Management

## Summary
Use enterprise power-management settings so computers and monitors move to low-power states automatically during inactivity and after hours.

## Estimated Savings
- **ASHRAE AEDG basis**: direct plug-load reduction in work-area equipment, one of the major office plug-load categories.
- **Typical effect**: strong operational savings where devices currently idle at full power outside active use.
- **End-Uses Affected**: workstation plug electricity and cooling.

## Basis / References
- AEDGZE Small to Medium Office, Chapter 5 plug-load management and equipment-selection guidance.

## Assumptions
Requires IT support, acceptable wake/sleep behavior, and differentiation between truly always-on systems and standard user devices.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Supports plug-load management policy and can reduce false demand for oversized electrical and cooling systems.

## Implementation Essentials
- Deploy centralized sleep, monitor-off, and standby settings through enterprise policy tools.
- Differentiate laptops, desktops, monitors, docking stations, and peripherals.
- Exclude critical devices only where clearly justified.
- Audit policy compliance after rollout.

## Risks / Constraints
- IT teams may resist aggressive sleep settings without testing.
- Users can defeat settings if wake/resume experience is poor.

## KPIs
- After-hours workstation load
- Policy compliance rate
- Average active versus standby workstation demand
- Help-desk tickets related to sleep/wake settings

## M&V Plan
- Use representative plug measurements or workstation fleet sampling where submetering is unavailable.
- Track after-hours baseload reduction in office areas after policy enforcement.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Low-cost operational/IT measure with typically favorable payback.

# ECM: Demand Side Response (DSR) / Grid Participation

## Summary

**What:** Participate in grid flexibility/DSR programmes (FFR, BM short-term operating reserve, or airport microgrid at Stansted) by curtailing non-critical loads in response to grid signals in exchange for revenue.

**Why:** At One Coleman Street and Radisson Blu Stansted (Airport microgrid), buildings have interruptible loads (chillers, lighting, EV chargers) that can be curtailed for grid services without impacting core operations.

## Estimated Savings
- Revenue stream (not energy savings); £10–50/kW/year
- Building-specific; £5,000–50,000/yr revenue potential

## Basis / References
- Portfolio basis: One Coleman Street, Radisson Blu Stansted (Airport microgrid)
- National Grid ESO FFR
- UK DSR aggregation market

## Assumptions
- Building-specific survey required before implementation
- Energy audit recommended to quantify baseline and savings

## Implementation Essentials
1. 1) Identify interruptible loads — equipment that can be shed without tenant comfort impact. 2) Install BMS integration for automatic load curtailment signals. 3) Register with DSR aggregator (e.g. Flexitricity, Open Energi). 4) Commission and test curtailment response. 5) Define fallback — manual override if automation fails.

## Risks / Constraints
- ⚠️ Requires interruptible loads with appropriate controls. Revenue streams vary — validate current market rates before investing in controls infrastructure.

## KPIs
- Curtailable load (kW)
- DSR events (#/month)
- Revenue (£/month)
- Curtailment response time (seconds)

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)
- Pre-installation baseline: 90-day continuous monitoring
- Post-installation: compare same period following year (weather-normalised)
- IPMVP Option C: Whole-building metering with regression analysis

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Payback: 6–18 months (controls infrastructure)
- Cost: Requires building-specific survey and contractor pricing

# ECM: Office Plug Load Management Policy

## Summary
Create and enforce a building plug-load management policy covering approved devices, shutdown expectations, and procurement standards.

## Estimated Savings
- **ASHRAE AEDG basis**: plug loads should be managed both by equipment selection and by ensuring devices are off when not needed.
- **Typical effect**: broad operational savings with especially strong effect on after-hours load.
- **End-Uses Affected**: plug electricity and cooling from internal gains.

## Basis / References
- AEDGZE Small to Medium Office recommends a plug load management policy as the first step to reducing office plug loads.
- The guide divides office plug loads into work areas, break rooms, and building process loads to support targeted action.

## Assumptions
Works only if ownership, tenants, IT, and FM teams align on allowed equipment and shutdown expectations.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Strengthens computer power management, controlled receptacles, and procurement-based ECMs.

## Implementation Essentials
- Define approved and prohibited device categories.
- Set after-hours shutdown expectations and exemptions.
- Integrate policy into tenant guides, fit-out standards, and procurement approval workflows.
- Review compliance periodically using interval electrical data.

## Risks / Constraints
- Policies without metering or accountability often decay quickly.
- Occupants may add personal devices if comfort or amenity gaps are unresolved.

## KPIs
- After-hours base load
- Count of nonapproved devices found during audits
- Plug-load intensity by tenant/floor
- Compliance rate with shutdown policy

## M&V Plan
- Use interval data to compare after-hours baseload before and after policy rollout.
- Supplement with periodic device audits.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Low-capex operational measure; strongest value when paired with metering and clear ownership.

# ECM: Daylight Harvesting Controls for Office Perimeter and Toplit Zones

## Summary
Use dimming or stepped controls linked to available daylight so perimeter and toplit office zones reduce electric lighting output automatically.

## Estimated Savings
- **ASHRAE AEDG basis**: project-specific lighting savings where daylight is real and not routinely blocked.
- **Typical effect**: often one of the most important control measures in zero-energy office design.
- **End-Uses Affected**: lighting primarily, with secondary cooling reduction.

## Basis / References
- AEDGZE Small to Medium Office includes dedicated sections for daylighting and lighting controls.
- The guide also includes sidebars on annual daylight metrics and luminaire-level controls.

## Assumptions
Requires usable daylight, correctly zoned luminaires, and calibrated sensors.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones; strongest in sunny climates and in offices with shallow floorplates or effective toplighting.

## Interaction Notes
Depends on glazing design, shading, reflectance, and occupant blind behavior.

## Implementation Essentials
- Zone luminaires by daylight availability, not arbitrary room boundaries alone.
- Use dimming systems where possible rather than simple switching.
- Place and calibrate sensors after furniture and partitions are known.
- Trend dimming fraction and overrides after occupancy.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor commissioning causes hunting or no meaningful savings.
- Tenant fit-out changes can invalidate original sensor tuning.
- Occupants may override systems if glare is not solved first.

## KPIs
- Lighting kWh in daylight zones
- Average dimming fraction
- Override frequency
- Daylight-zone hours at reduced output

## M&V Plan
- Use control trend logs as the primary verification method.
- Compare daytime lighting energy after commissioning where panel data are available.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Moderate controls cost with strong value in genuinely daylit offices.

# ECM: Controlled Receptacles and Smart Plug Strips

## Summary
Use controlled receptacles or smart plug strips to cut standby and after-hours energy from eligible office equipment.

## Estimated Savings
- **ASHRAE AEDG basis**: supports the “turn equipment off when not used” plug-load strategy.
- **Typical effect**: strongest for peripheral and discretionary loads not well managed by users.
- **End-Uses Affected**: plug electricity and after-hours baseload.

## Basis / References
- AEDGZE Small to Medium Office, Chapter 5 plug-load management guidance.

## Assumptions
Best for noncritical plug loads. Critical IT, life-safety, telecom, and always-on devices should remain exempt.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Works with occupancy scheduling and plug-load policy.

## Implementation Essentials
- Target desks, copy areas, meeting rooms, and break rooms where discretionary loads persist after hours.
- Separate always-on loads from controllable loads clearly.
- Use occupancy-, schedule-, or master-device-based control depending on zone type.
- Label controlled outlets clearly.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poorly chosen control points can interrupt devices users expect to remain live.
- Confusion about controlled versus uncontrolled outlets can create support issues.

## KPIs
- After-hours plug load in controlled circuits
- Override frequency
- User complaints related to receptacle control
- Standby load reduction

## M&V Plan
- Measure controlled-circuit or representative plug loads pre/post installation.
- Track after-hours load reduction and override behavior.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Low to moderate depending on electrical scope; often attractive when targeted rather than deployed indiscriminately.

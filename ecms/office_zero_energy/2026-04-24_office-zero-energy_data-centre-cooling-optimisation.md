# ECM: Data Centre Cooling & Power Optimisation

## Summary

**What:** Review server room/data centre cooling efficiency (PUE), airflow management (hot/cold aisle containment), and server utilisation. Significant standing load from cloud servers and legacy UPS. At One Coleman Street 3rd floor.

**Why:** Data centres often have PUE >1.8 due to poor airflow management and outdated cooling. Optimising reduces both energy cost and heat rejection load on building HVAC.

## Estimated Savings
- 10–30% data centre energy
- Building-specific

## Basis / References
- Portfolio basis: One Coleman Street (3rd floor)
- ASHRAE Thermal Guidelines for Data Processing Environments
- EU Code of Conduct for Data Centres

## Assumptions
- Building-specific survey required before implementation
- Energy audit recommended to quantify baseline and savings

## Implementation Essentials
1. 1) Measure current PUE (total building energy / IT equipment energy). 2) Conduct airflow analysis — thermal imaging of server racks. 3) Install hot/cold aisle containment. 4) Raise cold aisle temperature setpoint per ASHRAE A1 guidelines. 5. Engage IT team for server virtualisation and utilisation review. 6) Monitor PUE monthly post-optimisation.

## Risks / Constraints
- IT team ownership required — energy management teams may have limited authority over IT budget.

## KPIs
- PUE (target <1.4)
- Server intake temperature (°F)
- Cooling energy (kW)
- IT load (kW)

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)
- Pre-installation baseline: 90-day continuous monitoring
- Post-installation: compare same period following year (weather-normalised)
- IPMVP Option C: Whole-building metering with regression analysis

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Payback: 2–5 years
- Cost: Requires building-specific survey and contractor pricing

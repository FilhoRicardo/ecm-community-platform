# ECM: Sub-circuit Power Monitoring

## Summary

**What:** Clip-on CTs or smart breakers at distribution board level to monitor individual circuit loads — isolating HVAC, lighting, IT, and tenant loads. Enables targeted efficiency by identifying always-on or anomalous circuits.

**Why:** Whole-building electricity data masks which systems consume what. Sub-circuit monitoring at XYZ Building revealed always-on loads and anomalous overnight consumption patterns invisible at the main meter.

## Estimated Savings
- 3–8% (behavioural + targeted efficiency)
- Building-specific

## Basis / References
- Portfolio basis: XYZ Building

## Assumptions
- Building-specific survey required before implementation
- Energy audit recommended to quantify baseline and savings

## Implementation Essentials
1. 1) Prioritise panels serving highest-consumption systems (chillers, AHUs, data centres). 2) Install clip-on CT meters or smart breakers (ABB, Schneider, or GridX). 3) Configure data acquisition to EMS. 4) Establish baselines per circuit category. 5) Investigate always-on loads — setpoint schedules and equipment left running unnecessarily.

## Risks / Constraints
- Cost scales with panel count. Don't do blanket rollout — prioritise highest-consumption panels first.

## KPIs
- kW by circuit category
- Always-on load (kW, 24/7 baseline)
- Overnight kW (should be <30% of day)

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)
- Pre-installation baseline: 90-day continuous monitoring
- Post-installation: compare same period following year (weather-normalised)
- IPMVP Option C: Whole-building metering with regression analysis

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Payback: 8–18 months
- Cost: Requires building-specific survey and contractor pricing

# ECM: Tenant Engagement Programme

## Summary

**What:** Share energy consumption data with tenants via a portal or regular reports; provide best practice guidance on equipment use, out-of-hours consumption, and plug load management. At 5 Keizers, the Jumbo supermarket tenant warrants a standalone programme given disproportionate energy use and weekend/extended hours.

**Why:** Tenants control 30–70% of building energy consumption. Without feedback loops and guidance, behavioural waste (always-on equipment, out-of-hours HVAC, plug loads) goes unchecked.

## Estimated Savings
- 5–15% tenant controllable loads
- Building-specific

## Basis / References
- Portfolio basis: XYZ Building, 5 Keizers (Jumbo supermarket tenant)

## Assumptions
- Building-specific survey required before implementation
- Energy audit recommended to quantify baseline and savings

## Implementation Essentials
1. 1) Audit lease structure — identify green lease clauses, EPC requirements, tenant-controlled vs landlord-controlled systems. 2) Set up tenant energy portal (Green Building Engagement platform or quarterly PDF reports). 3) Provide plug load guidance — out-of-hours shutdown checklists. 4) At 5 Keizers: specific Jumbo engagement given disproportionate use and weekend operations. 5) Define shared KPI targets — align landlord and tenant incentives.

## Risks / Constraints
- Lease-dependent — if tenants pay their own energy bills directly, landlord influence is limited. Green lease clauses are leverage points.

## KPIs
- Tenant energy consumption (kWh/sq ft/yr)
- Out-of-hours equipment left on (kW)
- Green lease compliance (%)

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)
- Pre-installation baseline: 90-day continuous monitoring
- Post-installation: compare same period following year (weather-normalised)
- IPMVP Option C: Whole-building metering with regression analysis

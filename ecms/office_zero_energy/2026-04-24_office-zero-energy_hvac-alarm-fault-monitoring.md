# ECM: HVAC Alarm / Fault Monitoring

## Summary

**What:** Configure BMS (or standalone CAFM tool) to generate alerts for HVAC faults — stuck valves, failed sensors, compressor trips, filter alarms — and route to maintenance teams. At 5 Keizers, no alarm system exists.

**Why:** Stuck-open reheat valves and failed components can waste more energy over a year than most ECMs save, but without alarms, they run undetected for months. Proactive fault detection prevents savings degradation.

## Estimated Savings
- 3–8% (preventing degradation)
- Building-specific

## Basis / References
- Portfolio basis: 5 Keizers (no alarm system currently)

## Assumptions
- Building-specific survey required before implementation
- Energy audit recommended to quantify baseline and savings

## Implementation Essentials
1. 1) Audit current BMS alarm configuration — identify which faults generate alerts vs are silently logged. 2) Define alarm priority by energy impact: critical (stuck valves, failed compressors), warning (deviation from setpoint >X%), info (filter alarms). 3) Configure BMS alarm routing to maintenance mobile. 4) Set alarm thresholds — avoid alarm fatigue from excessive low-priority alerts. 5) Monthly alarm review meeting to identify recurring faults.

## Risks / Constraints
- ⚠️ Alarm fatigue is a real risk — too many alerts → ignored alerts. Prioritise by energy impact, not system default.

## KPIs
- Alarms generated (#/month)
- Fault resolution time (hrs)
- Energy impact of unresolved faults (kBtu/sq ft/yr)

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)
- Pre-installation baseline: 90-day continuous monitoring
- Post-installation: compare same period following year (weather-normalised)
- IPMVP Option C: Whole-building metering with regression analysis

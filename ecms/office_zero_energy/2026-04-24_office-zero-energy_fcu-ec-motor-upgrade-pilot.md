# ECM: Fan-coil Unit Efficiency Upgrade / Pilot Study

## Summary

**What:** Replace aged fan-coil motor/control boards with EC (electronically commutated) motor variants; run a pilot floor to quantify savings before full rollout. At SOM Building and UN Studio Tower, FCUs are a significant end-use.

**Why:** EC motor savings vs PSC motors are well-documented at 30–60% reduction in fan energy. But at building level, fan energy is typically 5–15% of total HVAC, so whole-building savings are 3–9%. Pilot validates the specific scenario before committing to full rollout.

## Estimated Savings
- 3–9% whole-building HVAC energy (30–60% FCU fan energy reduction)
- Building-specific

## Basis / References
- Portfolio basis: SOM Building, UN Studio Tower

## Assumptions
- Building-specific survey required before implementation
- Energy audit recommended to quantify baseline and savings

## Implementation Essentials
1. 1) Audit FCU population — age, motor type, running hours. 2) Select representative pilot floor (highest load or most accessible). 3) Replace PSC motors with EC motors on pilot FCUs. 4) Install metering on pilot floor circuits. 5) Measure 90-day baseline vs 90-day post-retrofit. 6) If pilot confirms savings, scope full building rollout.

## Risks / Constraints
- Pilot first. If BMS controls are poor, new efficient motors will be run harder to compensate — fix controls first (ECM-01).

## KPIs
- FCU fan kW (pilot floor)
- Whole-building HVAC kBtu/sq ft/yr
- Pilot ROI vs full rollout cost

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)
- Pre-installation baseline: 90-day continuous monitoring
- Post-installation: compare same period following year (weather-normalised)
- IPMVP Option C: Whole-building metering with regression analysis

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Payback: 2–5 years
- Cost: Requires building-specific survey and contractor pricing

# ECM: IoT Occupancy & Air Quality Sensors

## Summary

**What:** Deploy wireless IoT sensors (CO₂, PIR, temperature, humidity) at zone or room level, integrated into BMS or analytics platform (SavIQ, Gestalt). Enables CO₂-based DCV, occupancy-led HVAC, and IAQ monitoring.

**Why:** At XYZ Building and implicitly across the portfolio, zone-level sensing is absent or inadequate. DCV (ECM-11) and occupancy-led HVAC (ECM-12) both require room/zone-level sensor data.

## Estimated Savings
- Indirect (enabler of DCV and occupancy ECMs)
- Building-specific

## Basis / References
- Portfolio basis: XYZ Building

## Assumptions
- Building-specific survey required before implementation
- Energy audit recommended to quantify baseline and savings

## Implementation Essentials
1. 1) Define use cases before specifying — each sensor must answer a specific operational question. 2) Select wireless IoT platform (Distech, Siemens, or Wizzer) compatible with BMS. 3) Install CO₂ + PIR + temp sensors in representative zones (not all zones — sample). 4) Integrate data to BMS or EMS. 5) Commission: verify BMS receives data, set threshold alerts. 6) Budget for annual calibration and battery replacement.

## Risks / Constraints
- ⚠️ Sensor sprawl is expensive and wasteful without a clear use-case plan. Define decisions first, then specify sensors.

## KPIs
- CO₂ level by zone (ppm)
- Sensor battery life (months)
- BMS integration uptime (%)

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)
- Pre-installation baseline: 90-day continuous monitoring
- Post-installation: compare same period following year (weather-normalised)
- IPMVP Option C: Whole-building metering with regression analysis

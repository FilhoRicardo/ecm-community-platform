# ECM: Occupancy and Vacancy Lighting Controls

## Summary
Use occupancy or vacancy sensors in enclosed and intermittently used office spaces so lighting does not remain on when spaces are empty.

## Estimated Savings
- **Typical field guidance**: meaningful lighting reduction in enclosed offices, meeting rooms, restrooms, and support areas.
- **Best application**: spaces with intermittent use and frequent accidental runtime.
- **End-Uses Affected**: lighting electricity, with small secondary cooling impact.

## Basis / References
- AEDGZE Small to Medium Office, Chapter 5 Lighting Controls design strategies.
- Aligned with the user benchmark examples for occupancy-based interlocks and room-level optimization.

## Assumptions
Not every zone is suitable for aggressive occupancy shutoff. Savings depend on delay settings and occupancy patterns.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Overlaps with scheduling and luminaire-level controls.

## Implementation Essentials
- Use sensors in enclosed offices, focus rooms, meeting rooms, restrooms, copy rooms, and storage rooms.
- Prefer vacancy/manual-on logic where appropriate.
- Tune time delays to actual use patterns.
- Review placement after furniture layout and door swings are known.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor placement causes nuisance offs and user backlash.
- Savings can be eroded if occupants defeat sensors with overrides.
- Cleaning schedules must be coordinated.

## KPIs
- Lighting runtime by controlled zone
- Sensor overrides or complaint counts
- After-hours lighting profile
- Lighting kWh in controlled room types

## M&V Plan
- Compare zone runtime and panel energy pre/post installation or tuning.
- Use trend logs and override counts as the primary operational verification.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Typically low to moderate with favorable payback in intermittently occupied spaces.

# ECM: Heat Pump Water Heater for Office Domestic Hot Water

## Summary
Use heat pump water heating where office hot-water demand, installation conditions, and electrification goals support it.

## Estimated Savings
- **ASHRAE AEDG basis**: improvement in SWH efficiency, especially for electric pathways to zero energy.
- **Typical effect**: stronger where electric resistance would otherwise be used or where electrification is strategic.
- **End-Uses Affected**: service water heating electricity.

## Basis / References
- AEDGZE Small to Medium Office, Chapter 5 Service Water Heating includes heat-pump water heater performance requirements.

## Assumptions
Requires suitable ambient/source conditions, condensate handling, and a load profile that justifies storage-based heat-pump heating.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones, though economics and performance vary with source conditions and location.

## Interaction Notes
Pairs well with PV and right-sized DHW design.

## Implementation Essentials
- Screen load profile, storage volume, and installation location before selecting HPWH.
- Check noise, ventilation, and condensate requirements.
- Compare against gas-condensing and electric-resistance alternatives using lifecycle logic.
- Commission setpoints and recovery expectations.

## Risks / Constraints
- Poor siting can create noise or thermal side effects.
- Very peaky loads may require backup or larger storage than expected.

## KPIs
- DHW kWh
- HPWH COP or delivered efficiency
- Recovery time performance
- Hot-water availability complaints

## M&V Plan
- Use meter-based SWH energy comparison where feasible.
- Track delivered hot-water performance and electrical consumption post-commissioning.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Project-specific; stronger where electrification is strategic rather than purely cost-minimizing.

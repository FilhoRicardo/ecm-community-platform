# ECM: High-Efficacy LED Interior Lighting

## Summary
Use high-efficacy LED lighting systems to reduce connected load, improve controllability, and lower maintenance in offices.

## Estimated Savings
- **ASHRAE AEDG basis**: core reduction in lighting EUI as part of the zero-energy pathway.
- **Typical effect**: material reduction in lighting power and internal gains compared with older fluorescent or legacy LED designs.
- **End-Uses Affected**: lighting electricity and secondary cooling.

## Basis / References
- AEDGZE Small to Medium Office, Chapter 5 Electric Lighting covers interior lighting design strategies and space-specific strategies.
- The uploaded LED example provides the preferred style for savings framing, implementation, and M&V.

## Assumptions
Assumes design is based on delivered lighting quality rather than one-for-one fixture replacement.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones.

## Interaction Notes
Supports lower cooling loads and improves the value of daylight and occupancy controls.

## Implementation Essentials
- Specify high-efficacy fixtures with appropriate optics, CRI, glare control, and dimming compatibility.
- Use task/ambient design rather than blanket uniform lighting where appropriate.
- Coordinate fixture spacing and layout with workstation and partition planning.
- Commission scenes, dimming response, and sensor integration.

## Risks / Constraints
- Cheap fixtures can create glare, poor visual comfort, or driver issues.
- One-for-one replacement without redesign often leaves savings on the table.
- Occupants may add task lights if design quality is poor.

## KPIs
- Installed LPD
- Lighting kWh
- Complaint rate related to glare or darkness
- Driver/fixture failure rate

## M&V Plan
- Use panel-level or floor-level lighting energy comparison pre/post upgrade.
- Verify control functionality after commissioning.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Project-specific; often favorable lifecycle economics when integrated with control upgrades.

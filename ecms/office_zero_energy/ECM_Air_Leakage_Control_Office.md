# ECM: Continuous Air Barrier and Air Leakage Control

## Summary
Reduce infiltration and exfiltration through a clearly defined continuous air barrier and disciplined enclosure detailing.

## Estimated Savings
- **ASHRAE AEDG basis**: project-specific savings from reduced infiltration loads and improved controllability.
- **Typical effect**: often disproportionately valuable relative to cost because it improves both energy use and comfort.
- **End-Uses Affected**: heating, cooling, dehumidification, and perimeter comfort.

## Basis / References
- AEDGZE Small to Medium Office, Chapter 5 Envelope includes a dedicated air leakage control section.
- Chapter 4 identifies infiltration as a major simulation variable affecting office energy performance.

## Assumptions
Requires that the air barrier line be explicit in design documents and checked in the field.

## Climate Zone Relevance
All climate zones. Especially important in cold climates, hot-humid climates, and windy sites.

## Interaction Notes
Supports lower HVAC loads, better humidity control, and more stable perimeter comfort.

## Implementation Essentials
- Define one uninterrupted air barrier line for the entire enclosure.
- Detail continuity at slab edges, curtain-wall interfaces, roof edges, louvers, and service penetrations.
- Assign inspection responsibility during construction.
- Incorporate enclosure leakage testing where feasible.

## Risks / Constraints
- Responsibility gaps between trades are the most common failure mode.
- Late penetrations and field changes often undermine performance.
- Testing may be resisted unless included contractually.

## KPIs
- Measured leakage rate if tested
- Number of unresolved air-barrier deficiencies
- Humidity excursions in perimeter zones
- Heating and cooling EUI sensitivity to weather

## M&V Plan
- Prefer enclosure testing where practical.
- Otherwise use documented QA checklists, photos, and field inspection.

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Usually favorable because this is largely a detailing and QA measure rather than a major equipment purchase.

# ECM: UPS Right-sizing (Reduce Standing Losses)

## Summary

**What:** Review oversized UPS systems — large UPS at low load factors operate at poor efficiency (85–92% vs 96–98% at rated load). Right-sizing or replacing with modular UPS reduces standing losses.

**Why:** At One Coleman Street (3rd floor data centre), UPS is oversized for actual load. Every UPS efficiency point lost to low-load operation translates directly to wasted electricity 24/7.

## Estimated Savings
- 5–15% UPS energy; 2–5% whole-building electricity
- Building-specific

## Basis / References
- Portfolio basis: One Coleman Street (3rd floor data centre)

## Assumptions
- Building-specific survey required before implementation
- Energy audit recommended to quantify baseline and savings

## Implementation Essentials
1. 1) Commission full load study (kVA, kW, power factor) — measure actual load vs UPS rating. 2) Calculate current load factor and efficiency curve. 3) Specify right-sized UPS or modular UPS (allows incremental capacity). 4. Plan replacement with business continuity risk assessment. 5) Budget for electrical consultant, not just equipment spec.

## Risks / Constraints
- ⚠️ Critical infrastructure project with business continuity risk. Needs full load study before specifying replacements.

## KPIs
- UPS load factor (%)
- UPS efficiency (%)
- UPS kW consumption
- Whole-building electricity (kWh/month)

## M&V Plan (IPMVP Option C with submetering where feasible)
- Pre-installation baseline: 90-day continuous monitoring
- Post-installation: compare same period following year (weather-normalised)
- IPMVP Option C: Whole-building metering with regression analysis

## Costs & Payback (indicative)
- Payback: 3–7 years
- Cost: Requires building-specific survey and contractor pricing
